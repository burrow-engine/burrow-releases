Characters#

The player avatar and animated characters: package character (rig, skins, animation, .vox import, held items), character/charplay (the engine plugin: views, camera, scene nodes), the Character editor panel and the character scene component. Everything is frame domain (visual): it never writes simulation state, never changes StateHash or the journal, and the animation clock is the frame dt you give it, never the wall clock.

 physics / controller state  --(read only)-->  character.Motion  -->  Animator  -->  Pose  -->  part matrices
                                                                                            |
 skin (64x64 PNG) or .vox  -->  Rig (named parts, pivots, meshes)  ------------------------+--> rworld.MeshItem
                                                                         (render.extract input hook; soft AND Vulkan)

The rig#

A character.Rig is a hierarchy of named parts in world units: +Y up, the character faces -Z (camera yaw 0), its right hand is +X, the origin is the centre of the feet. Each Part has a role, a parent, a pivot (the joint position at rest) and a mesh whose vertices are relative to the pivot. Animated roles: torso, head, arm_r, arm_l, leg_r, leg_l. Any other part is a static child of its parent (a tail, a backpack). Parents precede children.

A Pose holds a root yaw/pitch/offset and a rotation (degrees: pitch X, yaw Y, roll Z) and an offset per role. Positive pitch swings a hanging limb forward and makes the head look up. Rig.Solve(root, pose) returns one object-to-world matrix per part.

Two model sources feed the same rig:

model idsourcelook
humanoidboxes + a 64x64 skin, 4 px arms1.8 blocks tall (one skin pixel = 1.8/32 block)
humanoid_slimsame, 3 px arms
voxel_herothe bundled voxel humanoid, 16 voxels tall
assets/x.voxany .vox split into named parts (below)scaled to 1.8 blocks, times scale

Skins (64x64)#

The format is the usual 64x64 RGBA layout: head 8x8x8, body 8x12x4, limbs 4x12x4 (3 px wide arms in the slim variant), an inner layer and an outer overlay layer (hat, jacket, sleeves, pants) drawn as a box 0.5 px larger and alpha masked (transparent texels show nothing). Each box is unfolded as

            U+D        U+D+W
   V        +--- top ---+-- bottom --+
   V+D  +---+---front---+---left-----+---back---+
        |right|  W x H  |   D x H    |  W x H   |

right is the character's right side (+X), left is -X, front is -Z. The layout table is character.BoxUV / FaceRect; the tests paint every face of every box with its own colour and check that the mesh samples it. character.SampleNearest is the reference nearest-texel sampler. Because both renderers filter bilinearly and choose mips from the pixel footprint, the texture handed to them is the skin enlarged 4x with nearest neighbour (SkinTexture), which keeps the blocky look.

Default skin. character.DefaultSkin draws an original skin ("Wanderer": cap, green tunic, red scarf) pixel by pixel from code; a golden PNG (character/testdata/default_skin.png) pins it. It is not derived from any third-party skin.

Importing a skin. Skins are plain PNG files: import one in the Character panel (Import PNG..., it must be exactly 64x64) or reference any project PNG in the component's skin field. New... writes a copy of the default skin into the project and opens it in the texture editor window, and Edit opens the current skin there (pixel grid, mirror drawing, layers); the preview picks the saved file up. No network download, no pack loader. A wrong size falls back to the default skin with a console warning. Skins of the old 64x32 layout are not supported.

Voxel characters (.vox)#

character.ImportVox splits a MagicaVoxel file into parts. In MagicaVoxel axes (Z up) the character faces -Y, so its right hand is at -X. Name each body piece (the object name in the scene panel, stored as the transform node's _name; groups are fine, the nearest named transform wins):

namemeaning
head, torso (or body), arm_r, arm_l, leg_r, leg_lthe animated parts (arm_r = the character's right)
forearm_r, forearm_l, shin_r, shin_loptional elbows and knees: the forearm hangs on its arm, the shin on its leg (joint: the top centre of the piece). The animator folds the knees backwards on each forward swing of the leg, bends the elbows (more on the forward swing of the arm, a lot when running), keeps them bent when crouching, jumping, falling, swimming or flying, and cocks the elbow in the attack and place clips. With forearm_r the held item sits at its front-bottom (pivot.hand still wins), and the first-person arm draws the forearm hanging from it
head.hat, arm_r.glovemore voxels merged into that part (<role>.<anything>)
tail@torsoa static extra part attached to torso (<name>@<role>)
pivot.head, pivot.arm_r, ...a marker object: its centre is the joint of that role
pivot.eye, pivot.handoptional eye and held-item anchors
(no name)merged into the torso

Without a pivot.* marker the joint is the top centre of arms and legs and the bottom centre of head and torso. The model is moved so its feet touch y=0, centred on the origin, and scaled to 1.8 blocks (times the component scale). character.SampleVoxHero() builds the bundled example; character.EncodeVoxParts writes it (examples/characters/voxel_hero.vox). A torso part is required.

Body plans: quadrupeds#

A rig has a body plan, humanoid (default, everything above) or quadruped (wolf, deer, sheep, horse...). Same axes (faces -Y, the animal's right is -X). The plan is detected as soon as one of the four legs below exists, or forced with VoxOptions.Plan ("humanoid" / "quadruped"; forcing maps arm_r, arm_l, leg_r, leg_l to leg_fr, leg_fl, leg_br, leg_bl and back).

namemeaning
torso (or body), headas for humanoids; the head hinges at its back-bottom
leg_fr, leg_fl, leg_br, leg_blfront/back, right/left legs (leg_fr = front right). Aliases: front_leg_r, foreleg_r, back_leg_l, hind_leg_l, hindleg_l...
tailhinged at its front end, sways and lifts
jaw (alias mouth, lower_jaw)hinged at its back-top, child of head; opens with the bite, eat, howl, rear clips
neckoptional; when present head hangs on it
ear_r, ear_loptional, children of head, flick at idle

A quadruped is 1 block tall by default (times scale); the held item attaches to the mouth. Animate it with character.NewAnimatorFor(rig) (or set Animator.Plan = rig.Plan): walk and crouch move the legs in diagonal pairs (leg_fr+leg_bl against leg_fl+leg_br), run is a bounding gallop with a lifted tail and panting jaw, idle sways the tail and breathes, jump and fall spread the legs. Head look is limited to +-55 degrees yaw and -35..+40 pitch. The attack and hurt triggers play bite and hurt_q on a quadruped. character.SampleVoxQuadruped() is the bundled reference fox (humanoid-only first-person arm code simply draws nothing for it).

Animation#

character.Animator is a deterministic state machine with blending. Step(dt, Motion) advances it:

m := character.Motion{Speed: 4.3, OnGround: true, Yaw: 90, Pitch: -10} // from the physics state + the camera
an.Step(1.0/60, m)
pose := an.Pose()

States, chosen by character.Classify (priority top to bottom): swim (Swimming), fly (Flying), jump (airborne and rising), fall, crouch, run (speed above 5.6 or sprinting), walk (speed above 0.15), idle. Poses are blended linearly over 0.12 s. The gait phase advances with the distance travelled (2.3 rad per block), so feet do not slide. Look: the head follows the camera pitch and yaw relative to the body (yaw clamped to 75 degrees); the body yaw follows the look yaw with exponential damping while moving or acting, and only drags along once the head is turned more than 50 degrees when standing still.

Clips are keyframed overlays (smoothstep between keys, fade in/out): attack (break), place, wave, hurt, cheer, nod; the locomotion state names are also valid clip names (they force that state, walking in place: the editor's preview). Animator.Trigger(name) plays a one-shot, Play(name, loop, rate) loops at a rate, Stop() fades out. Animator.Rate scales time.

Triggering from scripts and agents: set the anim field of the character component (editor_set_component, a script's Insert) to loop a named animation, "" to hand control back to the motion; Go hosts can also send the charplay.Trigger{Path, Name, Loop} engine event ("" path = the player). The player attack/place swings fire on the left/right mouse buttons.

Water. Motion.Swimming selects the swim animation. The player adapter derives it from app.FrameState.HeadUnder, InWater while airborne, or a liquid voxel at chest height; charplay.Avatar.Swimming replaces that test (the hook for other water physics). Scene characters use the position deltas of their node plus physics.character's on_ground/vy.

Rendering#

render.extract builds an rworld.Input from the scene; engine.AddRenderInputHook lets a plugin edit it before rworld.Extract. The character plugin appends MeshInstance items (one per part, plus the held object) with the texture and alpha-mask material, and CastShadow set, so the existing soft and Vulkan paths draw them (sun shadows on the terrain and on themselves where the renderer supports them) without any shader or render/vulkan change. Part meshes and textures live in a character.Store layered over the project assets (character.Overlay, keys start with char:).

Parity soft/Vulkan is measured by make character-test (Vulkan shot vs render/soft hybrid of the same frame, shaders off, mean abs RGB difference 0..255 over the pixels the character covers): third person walking 8.9, running from behind 6.2, voxel hero 4.7, first person 2.6 (the whole image, dominated by unrelated terrain differences, is 37 to 41: the soft frame has no load radius or fog). Tolerance 12.

Views and camera#

charplay.Plugin{Player: true} (burrowplay and the games enable it; runtime.plugins.character: false turns it off) draws the player and moves the camera:

  • First person: camera at the eyes; the right arm and the held object at the bottom right with a gait bob and the attack/place swing. The body and head are not drawn.
  • Third person back / front: the whole body; the camera sits dist blocks behind (or in front of, looking back at) the eye, shortened by a voxel raycast so it never goes through terrain.

Input actions (in the default input map; custom maps add them to opt in): camera_toggle (F5: first, back, front), camera_closer / camera_farther (PageUp/PageDown, =/-), camera_zoom (Ctrl + wheel), crouch (C, visual only: the sandbox controller has no crouch physics). Rebind them in vox.project's input map like any action. F5 used to toggle block textures in the window; while the avatar plugin is on it belongs to the camera (engine.KeyReservations).

The held object is the selected hotbar block: a cube with the blocktex face textures and the same plains biome tint as the 3D hotbar icons (blockicon.FaceImage); a block without a texture uses its flat colour; a non-block item (charplay.Avatar.Held = &character.Held{Kind: character.HeldItem}) is a generic tool placeholder. The game hotbar keeps its 3D block icons. Project blocks (runtime.custom_blocks) show their own PNG faces too (a texture reload refreshes the held atlas).

Precedence of the local player's held object, highest first: charplay.Avatar.Held (Go), held_model of the Avatar child's character component, a non-empty held of that component (a block name; a game with its own inventory sets it from a script, empty gives the hotbar back), the engine hotbar block.

Walking sway: the speed that drives the walk cycle is measured over 0.2 s (0.5 s for scene characters), so the arm does not jitter at any frame rate.

Your own character, from scratch#

Use data, never a script, for the body, the arm and the hand:

  1. Draw a .vox with named parts (head, torso, arm_r, arm_l, leg_r, leg_l, optional pivot.hand) or a 64x64 skin, and set it on the Avatar child of your player prefab variant (model, skin, scale).
  2. The first-person arm is that model's arm_r; tune it with fp_offset, fp_rot and fp_scale.
  3. Hold a custom object with held_model (a .vox), or leave the hotbar block (textured, tinted like the icons).

From an empty project (burrow new mygame --template empty), write assets/prefabs/hero_player.prefab:

{"version":1,"nodes":[{"id":1,"name":"Player","components":{"prefab.instance":{"prefab":"builtin:player.prefab","overrides":[
  {"path":"Avatar","component":"character","fields":{"model":"assets/hero.vox","held_model":"assets/tool.vox","fp_offset":[0.3,-0.25,-0.3],"fp_rot":[60,10,0],"fp_scale":0.8}}]}}}]}

then burrow config set runtime.player_prefab assets/prefabs/hero_player.prefab, check with burrow test and look at the arm and the held object with burrowplay -shot out.png -frames 30 -freeze (run in the project folder; the player starts in first person).

The arm and the held object are drawn in the render hook with the interpolated camera, so they never jitter. A script updates a node at the simulation rate (20 Hz): do not move a node from a script to fake a viewmodel, and do not push blocks to the hotbar to show them in hand.

The character component#

{"id": 2, "name": "Hero", "components": {
  "transform": {"pos": {"x": 0, "y": 9, "z": 0}, "rot": {"x": 0, "y": 0, "z": 0}, "scale": {"x": 1, "y": 1, "z": 1}},
  "character": {"model": "humanoid", "skin": "assets/skins/me.png", "scale": 1, "held": "stone", "anim": "", "speed": 1, "hidden": false}}}

model: humanoid, humanoid_slim, voxel_hero or a .vox asset path; skin: a 64x64 PNG asset (empty = built-in); scale: 0.05..20 (0 = 1); variant: the layer set of a layered .vox / .bmodel model (antlers, a collar, shorn wool: each part is built from the variant's replacement model; empty or unknown = default); recolor: palette changes of a .vox model (3=#c02020,7=#2060d0, see modeling.md) for colour variants of one animal or villager without a new file; held: a block name; held_model: a .vox asset held instead of a block (its longest side is 1 block, times held_scale; with two marker instances pivot.grip (where the fist closes) and pivot.tip (along the handle, towards the working end) it is held like a tool: the grip in the fist, the handle forward and up, the prop's x axis (its width) in the vertical plane of the handle, HeldGripSize 0.62 block long times held_scale; the markers are not drawn; held_angle sets the handle's angle above the horizontal, 0 = 25 degrees, 70 for a torch carried up; a pivot.fx marker plus held_fx (a particle effect such as builtin:fire) and held_fx_scale put that effect at the marker every frame, through particles.FrameEmitters: a torch burns in the hand); fp_offset / fp_rot / fp_scale: the first-person arm and held object in camera space (right, up, forward is -Z; rotation in degrees pitch, yaw, roll; all zero = the defaults 0.30,-0.20,-0.20, 70,16,-6, 0.7); fp_sway / fp_bob: multipliers of the first-person walking arm swing and bob (0 = 1, negative = off, max 3; the swing of an attack or place clip is not scaled); anim: see above; speed: animation rate (0 = 1); hidden. It is a normal schema component: saved in .voxscn, validated (unknown animation or a negative scale is refused), shown by the inspector with tooltips and ranges, undoable, and editable by the editor agent with the generic tools (editor_create_node with components, editor_set_component, editor_remove_component); no dedicated MCP tool is needed.

Registering a component is part of the simulation configuration and changes StateHash. To keep the hashes of existing projects, a game registers character only when runtime.plugins.character is explicitly true or its default scene uses it (the editor always registers it). The avatar and the animation themselves never change the hash (test TestAnimationDoesNotTouchStateHash).

Fill light#

At low sun a character can turn into a black silhouette. env.post fill_light (0..2, see lighting.md) adds a real light to the character meshes (character.Subject.Fill): a warm directional fill from the camera side (above and slightly left of the view) plus a cool rim (back light, fresnel-like edge, pow(1 - n.v, 3)), both scaled by the strength and added to the lighting before albedo. It lives in the built-in mesh shaders (mesh.frag, mesh_hdr.frag; the strength travels in Material.Params[0], so materials with a custom shader ignore it), costs a few ALU per pixel and does not touch the simulation. It applies to scene characters and to the player avatar (third person and the first-person arm). The soft renderer draws no characters. Pair it with exposure for a menu backdrop.

Editor panel#

Tools > Character editor is a full-screen tool like the model editor. The header shows the node, undo/redo, the view (Orbit, First person, Back, Front), Turntable, Frame (F) and a menu. The rail on the left has Select a part (V: click a part of the preview), Pose (R: drag rotates the selected part; an editor-only pose, never saved, with a Reset pose chip), Paint the skin (P: opens the skin in the texture editor) and Attach a held item (H: pick the held block). The centre is the isolated 3D preview (left or right drag orbits, middle drag or Shift+right drag pans, wheel zooms, F frames it; drawn by the GPU at the panel's own pixel size, with the render/soft CPU renderer only for headless runs) above the Animations strip: a card per clip (length, loop, progress of the playing one), a click plays it, and Open in the animation editor. Character settings has three tabs: Body (model cards for the built-ins and the project's .vox assets, scale, height, eye height, preview walk speed, held items), Skin (64x64 thumbnail, Edit, New, Import) and Motion (walk speed, animation speed, loop). With no character node selected the preview still works and an empty-state card offers Create character node / Add to selection. Every change to a node is one undo step.

Limits#

  • The first-person arm is depth tested against the terrain, so it clips into a wall you press against.
  • No inverse kinematics, no foot planting on slopes, no cloth or secondary motion, no per-part tint, no capes.
  • The sandbox controller has no crouch or swim physics of its own: crouch is visual, swim follows FrameState.
  • Skins are 64x64 only (no 64x32 legacy skins) and are not downloaded.
  • The player is rendered from the controller state of the frame: another player or NPC needs a character node.
  • The Vulkan shadow of the character depends on the renderer's sun shadow settings (unchanged here).

The player is an entity#

The first person player is a node of the scene, an instance of a prefab you can override.

Defining the player (one path)#

A player is game.mode (what the scene wants) plus a player prefab (what the player is). Everything else is a fallback for projects without a game.mode; first match wins.

Question1. Scene2. Project3. Engine
Which prefab?explicit scene_spawn_player.prefabgame.mode.player_prefab, then runtime.player_prefabbuiltin:player.prefab
Where?game.mode.spawns + spawn_tag (picked by spawn_pick), then game.mode.spawnruntime.startabove the terrain at the origin

scene.ResolveSpawn and scene.PlayerPrefabPath are the only implementations; the controller, a scene switch and a session join all call them. A node you build by hand with physics.character and a script is a creature, not a player: it gets no camera, input or capabilities. Give the scene a game.mode and a prefab variant instead. All three walkers (the player, physics.character and remote players) are one function, physics/walker.Step, with the same defaults: gravity 32, terminal speed 78, jump 1.25 blocks, sneak edge reach 0.6; a player steps 1.05 (only where shaped blocks exist), a creature takes its own step_height.

  • Prefab. The player is spawned from builtin:player.prefab (engine prefab, shipped in the binary; builtinprefabs.List()): a root Player with the player component (id, mode walk/fly, eye) and the tag player, and a child Camera with an active render.camera at eye height. A scene chooses another with the game.mode component (player_prefab, spawn); without one, runtime.player_prefab in vox.project applies, else the built-in. A project prefab is usually a variant: a file holding one node with a prefab.instance link to builtin:player.prefab and overrides (for example {"path":"Camera","component":"transform","fields":{"pos":[0,2,0]}}); add scripts or children with add overrides.
  • Spawn. The controller spawns player 0 on its first frame (journaled scene_spawn_player) at game.mode.spawn (a node path; its position and yaw), else runtime.start, else above the terrain. In a session every PlayerJoin spawns one instance (Player<id>, inactive camera) and a leave despawns it. A scene switch keeps the players and respawns them from the new scene's prefab and spawn point. In the editor, Play spawns the node and Stop discards it.
  • Pose. In single player the controller mirrors its feet position, yaw, pitch and mode into the node with the journaled, quantized scene_player_pose command, so the node is hashed, replayed and snapshotted. In a session the pose stays frame domain (each peer owns its camera); the node stays at the spawn point. The view itself uses the controller's interpolated pose and FOV (the player camera node is rendered with app.FrameState), so camera rigs keep working. The camera height comes from the prefab's Camera node.
  • Scripts. burrowsdk.Player(id) / burrowsdk.LocalPlayer() return the node; FindTag("player", 0) finds all.
  • Avatar. The prefab has a child Avatar with a character component (model, skin, scale). A variant changes the model with an override ({"path":"Avatar","component":"character","fields":{"model":"voxel_hero"}}). The character plugin draws the body from that node (mesh entities are the node, so the editor selects it), driven by the player's animator; first person hides it. A prefab without an Avatar child falls back to the frame-domain avatar at the controller's pose. The character component is registered by default (runtime.plugins.character: false turns it off; the built-in prefab then loads without it).

Capabilities#

What the player may do is data on the player node, read every frame by the controller (all hashed, so a replay agrees). The absent component means the capability is off; builtin:player.prefab carries every one with today's behaviour, and a variant overrides fields or removes the component. A player without a node (bare simulations) keeps everything on.

ComponentFields
player.movewalk, fly, walk_speed, fly_speed, sprint, sprint_mul, crouch, jump, jump_height, step_height, gravity_scale, swim, fly_toggle (both/key/double_tap/none), no_climb (ladders and vines cannot be climbed). A zero number takes the controller default. Neither walk nor fly: the player cannot move.
player.buildbreak, place, reach, blocks (names that may be placed, empty = any), protect (names that cannot be broken), repeat (hold to repeat)
player.interactblock (doors, buttons, block logic), node (nodes with interact), reach
player.inventoryslots (0..9 usable hotbar slots), creative (the creative panel), items (block names of the first slots at spawn)
player.controlsinput_map (project path of an input map)
player.impulsevel (m/s), seq; not on the built-in prefab, a script adds it (see Impulses)

Climbing. Inside a ladder, vine or rope cell (a non-solid block whose name contains ladder, vine or rope: the rule nav agents use with can_climb) a walking player goes up at 2.35 m/s with forward or jump, down with back or the down key, holds its height while sneaking and otherwise slides down at 2 m/s at most, slower than a hard landing, so no landing hit or fall damage follows. player.move.no_climb turns it off.

Entities. The controller collides with the voxels and, once wired with entcollide, with solid entities: physics.body (not triggers), physics.character creatures and physics3d rigid bodies and voxel bodies, each as an axis-aligned box (rotated bodies by their rotated extent). Sweeps stop at the first box face (no tunnelling), a box that overlaps the player is pushed out along the shortest axis (at most 0.15 m per physics step), and you can stand on a box. The player node and its children never collide with the player, nor does a node tagged player_passthrough. Wire it with ctl.SetEntityBoxes(entcollide.Source(engine, schema)) (package app/entcollide; app itself cannot import physics).

Impulses. player.impulse pushes the player: write vel (m/s, x y z) and a new seq and the controller adds vel to its velocity once per seq (knockback, jump pads). From Go scripts burrowsdk.PlayerImpulse(player, [3]float64{0, 9, 0}) bumps the seq for you. The component is hashed and replayed like the other player data, and a project that never writes it keeps its state hash. A controller that first sees a node only records its seq, so a loaded game does not replay an old push.

Controls. The input map in use is the local player's player.controls.input_map, else the scene's game.mode.input_map, else the project's input_map. Switching scene or player prefab switches the map (input/controls, frame domain).

Spawn points (game.mode): spawns (node paths) and spawn_tag, spawn_pick (round_robin by player id | random, seeded by the engine seed | farthest from the other players).

Health, death and respawn are the game's rules, not the engine's. The engine has no hit points, lives, respawn delay or kill plane (player.health and the lives, respawn_delay, respawn_at, kill_plane and kill_y fields of game.mode were removed, along with the scene_player_hurt/die/respawn/checkpoint/rules commands and the player.died and player.respawned events). Keep the values in the game's own state (a Go game: Ctx.State), decide in your tick, and put the player back with a pose (scene_player_pose, Ctx.SetPos). Scenes and prefabs saved with the removed component or fields still load: they are dropped on load, and burrow migrate removes them from the files. The level settings.kill_y and bounds of the levels plugin return an actor that leaves the level to the level's default spawn (a plain teleport).

Scene switch. Players survive a switch. A scene root with the same name as a kept player (for example Player) is renamed Player_scene (then Player_scene2, ...) when it loads; the player keeps its name.

Hooks for sound and effects (frame domain, on app.Controller): OnFootstep(pos, ground, sprinting) every ~1.9 voxels walked, OnJump, OnLand(pos, ground, speed), OnBreak, OnPlace. Player id 0 is the local player; in a session it is the netsim player id (Controller.PlayerID).

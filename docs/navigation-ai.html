Navigation and AI#

Package nav (graph, pathfinding, steering, plugin) and nav/ai (behaviour trees, state machines, utility scoring, perception geometry). Genre neutral: a profile describes the body, the voxels describe the world.

It is off by default in vox.project (runtime.plugins.nav: true enables it: its components and request queue are hashed state), or add &nav.Plugin{} after ScenePlugin, the voxel world and PhysicsPlugin. Behaviour definitions load from <name>.bt.json (or ai/<name>.bt.json) in the project. Try it: go run ./examples/nav_demo -shot out.png (CPU renderer, no window).

Design#

  • Graph. A node is the voxel holding an agent's feet (for width 2, the lowest corner of its 2x2 footprint). Per profile (Height 1-8, Width 1-2, StepHeight, JumpHeight <= 4, DropHeight <= 31 (a region reads at most one chunk below a node), CanSwim, CanClimb, CanFly, NoDiagonal) a node is ground (solid floor, body clear), water (feet in liquid, or floating on it), climb (ladder, vine and rope blocks, World.Climbable) or air (flyers: any clear cell). Moves: 4/8 neighbours with step up, jump up, drop down (fall column clear), unit moves through water and ladders, 26 neighbours without corner cutting for flyers. Costs are tenths of a block: 10 / 14, +4 per stepped block, +14 per jumped block, +2 per dropped block, 15 swimming, 20 climbing. The heuristic is admissible, so flat A* is optimal (tests compare it with BFS and Dijkstra).
  • Micro-voxels. A cell holding a brick (docs/blocks.md "Micro-voxels") is neither solid nor air: its solid micro voxels (registry Solid) are reduced to a mask of sixteenths of the cell height inside the agent's footprint band (the whole cell, or the centred 2 * RadiusM band for a one-cell wide agent with RadiusM, so a doorway carved 8/16 wide passes a 0.5 m wide agent and not a 1 m one). An agent stands at the lowest feet offset f (0..15 sixteenths) with a solid layer right under its feet (the cell below for f = 0) and its body, Height cells tall, free above them: on a micro slab or half-step it stands at f = 8, under a lintel only if it fits. Moves compare floors in sixteenths: a rise up to the step height (StepHeight, or StepM in sixteenths of a voxel after Scaled) is a step (cost +4 per voxel, rounded up per micro step), up to the jump height a jump, a micro step down is a walk, a longer fall a drop. TestWalkerCrossesMicroStepsAndEndsOnTheSlab drives a nav agent that is a physics.character over a micro quarter step and a micro slab and checks it ends at the slab height; the character reads the world through sim.Chunks (voxel.Access), which exposes the bricks, the shape registry and the fine water like the map itself. A world without bricks classifies and costs exactly as before (navmesh results unchanged). String pulling only joins nodes with the same feet offset. The follower aims at the micro floor: the waypoint height is the node's cell plus its feet offset (feetY), so the arrival test, the final approach, the jump decision (a rise above the controller's step height) and the kinematic height all use the sixteenths, and an agent steps up micro steps smoothly and stands on a micro slab. Water in bricks: a brick cell counts as liquid for the swim, avoid and surface-float rules when any of its micro voxels holds a liquid block (liquidAt; the layer does not matter, the body of an agent in the cell is wet), so a swimmer treats it as water and a walker cannot stand in it. Fine water: when the source carries the fluid automaton's fine bricks (voxel.FineReader: a ChunkMap with SetFine, or voxel.Access), a cell whose fine brick holds a mean depth of at least World.FineWaterDepth (a fraction of the cell, default 0.5, negative disables) is liquid too. It is live state read on each liquidAt query, not cached with the chunk, so cached region paths are not invalidated when the water moves.
  • Derived, never state. World classifies each chunk into solid/liquid/climbable bit planes (12 KB per chunk) and validates it against voxel.Chunk.Version (changes on every write, undo restore and decode; new, additive accessor) on first use per request. A stale answer is impossible, with or without an observer, after undo and after snapshot restore. The voxel.Observer feed (World.Attach, chained behind an existing observer such as fluid's) only queues changed chunks: World.Update(budget) rebuilds at most budget of them per call in ascending chunk order. Tests prove incremental update == fresh rebuild (classification and regions) and that undo is noticed. The caches are bounded (MaxCachedChunks 2048, MaxCachedRegions 1024, least recently touched dropped at the start of the next request: wall time only, charges are unchanged). Detach is chain-aware (an observer installed after ours stays in the chain); install fluid before Attach, since fluid refuses a map that already has an observer.
  • Hierarchy. For goals farther than 48 blocks, each chunk is split per profile into regions (components of the mutual moves inside the chunk) with portals (moves leaving the chunk). An abstract A* over regions gives a corridor, then every chunk segment is refined by a small A* confined to that chunk (HPA). The abstract search, the refinement and a fallback flat A share one unit budget (an unreachable goal in a big loaded world costs the budget, not hundreds of region builds), and every portal move is re-checked before it is used. Any failure falls back to flat A. On a 200x200 field with pillars and walls the corridor search expanded 746 nodes against 2275 for flat A at 5 % longer paths.
  • Smoothing. String pulling: look-ahead 24 nodes, a straight sweep of the body (corner cells included) must be free; ground profiles only smooth within one level, flyers and swimmers in 3D.
  • Flying and swimming. Flyers use 3D flat A* (no hierarchy: long flights are partial paths that re-plan). Swimmers switch between walking and swimming nodes; a swimming physics.character gets the swim intent (Move.Y).
  • Dynamic obstacles. nav.obstacle entities block a box each tick (not cached, not in the hierarchy). Moving or removing one changes the answer on the next request; agents revalidate the next four waypoints every ValidateEvery ticks and re-plan when a node vanished.
  • Crowds. Steer is RVO-lite: 28 candidate velocities (7 headings at 3 speeds + stand still) scored by deviation and time to collision against up to Neighbours nearest agents (spatial hash, fixed order, pure function), plus a personal-space push. Neighbour velocities are the previous tick's, so entity order never matters.
  • Following. Agents with a physics.character get Move/Jump intent (jump when the next waypoint is above the step height, swim up/down in water); others are moved by the plugin (flyers, simple creatures). Stuck for 3 s: re-plan.

Determinism and budgets#

Paths are computed inside the fixed tick by the system nav (before physics.character) from a FIFO queue (Queue, a hashed fixed resource: entity + goal sequence). Requests run to completion, never across ticks, so there is no half-done search to snapshot. The cost of a request is counted in units (one unit is about one node expansion; first touch of a chunk = 4, of a region = 100, per request) and charged whether or not the data was cached, so which tick a path completes in never depends on the cache. engine.VerifyCacheNeutrality passes with the plugin.

capdefaultmeaning
TickUnits4000units spent on requests per tick (one request may overshoot by its last step, <= ~1000)
ReqUnits2500cap of one request; beyond it the best partial path is returned
MaxPerTick48requests started per tick
MaxQueue4096queued requests; agents beyond stay pending and retry the next tick
ValidatePerTick, ValidateEvery256, 8path revalidations
ScanPerTick, ThinkPerTick96, 256perception scans and brain thinks per tick: the agents whose next time is oldest go first, so nobody starves
ScanChunks32chunks re-verified per tick by the prewarm
Neighbours6crowd neighbours per agent

Per agent and tick the plugin always does O(1) work (follow, steer, write back changed components only). Measured on this machine: 10k agents with every request queued: worst tick 7 ms; 3000 walking agents with crowd avoidance: mean tick 3.5 ms (see nav/scale_slow_test.go, go test -tags slow ./nav). A far request (170 blocks) costs ~1800 units, about 1.4 ms warm and 20 ms the first time the chunks are seen. Agents under a parent node use their world position (WorldAffine); nav writes back the local position. Limits: coordinates within +-1048575; a stored path holds at most 512 waypoints (longer ones are partial and re-planned); unloaded space counts as air (no floor, so not walkable, but flyers may enter it).

Commands (journaled, in the agent session act list and engine.RegisterCommand): nav_set_goal {path, goal, slack}, nav_stop {path}. nav.SpawnAgent(name, pos, agent, extra...) returns the commands that create an agent node. nav.Res(app).Stats() reports the last tick (agents, requests, units, queue, scans, thinks, builds).

a.AddPlugin(&nav.Plugin{Config: nav.Config{
	Profiles: []nav.Profile{nav.Walker, nav.Flyer, {Name: "tank", Height: 3, Width: 2, StepHeight: 1, JumpHeight: 1, DropHeight: 2}},
	Assets:   func(name string) ([]byte, error) { return os.ReadFile("ai/" + name + ".bt.json") },
}})
cmds := nav.SpawnAgent("wolf", mathx.Vec3{X: 4.5, Y: 1.9, Z: 4.5}, nav.NewAgent("walker"),
	nav.Comp{Name: physics.CompCharacter, Value: physics.NewCharacter()},
	nav.Comp{Name: nav.CompBrain, Value: nav.NewBrain("wolf")})

Without the physics plugin agents are moved by nav itself, so a headless game needs nothing else. With it, give the character StepHeight >= profile.StepHeight + 0.05.

Voxel size#

Everything an author writes is in metres; the graph is built in voxels. At the default one metre voxel the two are the same, so existing profiles and components read unchanged. In a world of n voxels per metre (0.5 m, 0.25 m, 0.125 m voxels: n = 2, 4, 8) the plugin converts at Build (Profile.Scaled(n)): the whole-number fields (Height, Width, StepHeight, JumpHeight, DropHeight) multiply by n, and the optional fractional fields HeightM, RadiusM, StepM, JumpM, DropM (JSON height_m, radius_m, step_m, jump_m, drop_m) win over them. Rounding keeps the body honest: height and footprint (2 * RadiusM in cells) round up, so a 1.8 m agent never fits a gap lower than itself; step, jump and drop round down, so it never claims a ledge or fall beyond its ability; drop is capped at 31 voxels. Body limits scale with n (height 8 m, width 2 m, jump 4 m). A 1.8 m / 0.4 m agent (HeightM 1.8, RadiusM 0.4) is 2x1 voxels at scale 1 and 8x4 at scale 4, and passes the same 2 m doors in either. Build graphs by hand with NewWorldAt(n, profile.Scaled(n)).

nav.agent speed, radius and slack, ai.perception sight, hear and eye, nav.target height, and the behaviour tree arguments (wander radius, chase stop, flee dist, dist_lt/dist_gt) are metres; sensed distances (seen[].dist, target_dist) are reported in metres. Path costs are tenths of a voxel of walking and the ratios between move kinds are per voxel, so paths have the same shape at every size; nav.CostMetres(cost, n) converts a result cost to metres. goal and path stay voxel cells.

Behaviour trees and state machines (nav/ai)#

A definition is JSON (.bt.json) or Go (ai.Def). Node types: sequence, selector (both resume the running child; "reactive": true re-evaluates from the first child every think and resets the child it pre-empts), parallel (policy all or any), inverter, succeeder, failer, repeat {n}, retry {n}, cooldown {seconds}, timeout {seconds}, wait {seconds}, condition {name,args}, action {name,args}, utility (each child has a score naming a registered scorer; the best child runs until it finishes). Limits: 512 nodes, depth 32, 1 MiB.

{"type":"bt","name":"wolf","root":{"type":"selector","reactive":true,"children":[
  {"type":"sequence","children":[{"type":"condition","name":"has_target"},{"type":"action","name":"chase","args":{"stop":1.6}}]},
  {"type":"sequence","children":[{"type":"action","name":"wander","args":{"radius":9}},{"type":"wait","seconds":1}]}]}}

{"type":"fsm","fsm":{"initial":"graze","states":[{"name":"graze","on_tick":[{"name":"stop"}],"transitions":[{"to":"alert", "if":[{"name":"can_see"}]}]}, ...]}} is a state machine: on_enter and on_tick calls run, then the first transition whose conditions (not negates) all succeed and that is at least after seconds old fires. examples/nav_demo/ai/ has a tree and a machine for each creature.

Built-in conditions: has_target, can_see, heard, arrived, path_failed, moving, in_water, bb_true {key}, bb_lt/bb_gt {key,v}, dist_lt/dist_gt {v,key}, chance {p}. Actions: wander {radius}, move_to {key,slack}, look_home, chase {key,stop}, flee {key,dist}, stop, set {key,value}, idle. Scorers: const, random, target_near, target_far. Register your own with nav.Config.AI (ai.NewRegistry().Func(name, f).Scorer(...)); a function gets an *ai.Ctx (blackboard, tick, Rand, Local scratch) and nav.ViewOf(ctx) for the agent.

Everything an agent remembers is in ai.brain: the blackboard (sorted, at most 64 keys; numbers, vectors, entities and short strings) and mem, so snapshots, undo and replay cover an agent in the middle of an action. ctx.Rand is a pure function of agent, tick and salt. The blackboard keys target, target_pos, target_dist, can_see, heard, arrived, failed, moving, self_pos and home are refreshed before every think. A bad asset name or definition leaves the brain inert (a failed read is retried after 60 ticks, a failed compile after 600; the tick never fails). The brain stores the content hash of its asset (ai.brain.hash): a changed asset restarts the brain, and an asset that differs between a live run and its replay shows as a StateHash difference. Config.Assets must be a pure function of the name during a run. State machines are capped at 256 calls/transitions and 8 conditions per transition; forged mem values never crash a tick. .bt.json files are project files and asset database assets (importer behaviour, validated at load); the plugin compiles them on first use through Config.Assets.

Perception. ai.perception scans every interval ticks (at most ScanPerTick per tick): targets within max(sight, hear) from a spatial hash, nearest first, at most 12 checked. Sight: inside the cone (fov, facing follows movement), within sight, and a voxel.Raycast from the eye to the target's height is not blocked. Hearing: distance <= hear * noise (through walls). Seen targets are remembered memory ticks, nearest first, at most 8.

Utility scoring. ai.Option{Considerations: [...]} with ai.Curve response curves (linear, quadratic, sqrt, inverse, logistic, step) scored with a compensation factor; ai.Best picks the first maximum.

Scripts#

Additive host calls (module vox, see script/ABI.md) and sdk/burrowsdk/nav.go: NavSetGoal, NavStop, NavSetGoalPos (world position), NavGetState, NavPath, NavSetBB, NavGetBB (numbers), NavPutBB, NavReadBB, NavDelBB (typed values: number, vector, entity, string), NavSensed. They edit the nav components like entity_set_component (applied at the end of the invocation, journaled).

Debug overlay and tools#

engine.DebugLines (engine/debuglines.go) is a frame-domain list of world lines in named layers (Layer("nav"); a user only ever clears its own layer). nav.Res(app).SetDebug(nav.DebugOptions{Paths: true, Graph: true, Center: c, Radius: 12}) makes the plugin fill its layer every frame: paths (green, white waypoints, red goal box), the walkable graph (yellow ground, blue water, magenta ladder, cyan air), obstacles (orange). The CPU renderer and -shot captures draw the lines with engine.OverlayLines; the Vulkan renderer draws them live (render/vulkan/lines.go, shaders dbgline.*, up to 4096 lines per frame, depth tested) in the game window and in the editor viewport (make nav-demo-gpu writes a GPU frame).

The editor and the editor MCP server have it too: the Project settings dialog has a nav checkbox (runtime.plugins.nav; burrowedit -nav), and the tool editor_nav {action state|path|goal|stop|debug} queries a path over the current world, sends a nav.agent node to a goal (journaled nav_set_goal), stops it and toggles the overlay.

Tests#

go test ./nav/...: mazes against BFS and Dijkstra, incremental update against a fresh rebuild, undo and stale caches, hierarchy validity and cost, step/jump/drop/headroom, swim/climb/fly, obstacles, steering, behaviour tree semantics, JSON loading, twin runs, snapshot mid path, journal replay, cache neutrality, perception, path floods (600 agents fast, 10k with -tags slow), fuzzing (go test ./nav -fuzz FuzzFindPath, ./nav/ai -fuzz FuzzLoad).

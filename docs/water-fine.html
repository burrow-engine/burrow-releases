Fine water (adaptive sub-cell bricks)#

The coarse automaton (water-physics.md) moves water in eighths of a block, four cells per second, with no volume: a source is infinite and a flowing cell drains when it is not fed. The fine water is the default replacement (runtime.plugins.fine_water: false keeps the coarse automaton) that keeps a real, conserved volume of water at a resolution of 1/N block (N = 2, 4 or 8, default 4), only where water moves. Calm water folds back into ordinary blocks, which remain the truth for large still volumes.

coarse (W1, projects not yet upgraded)fine (Config.Fine)
stateblocks water, water_1..7blocks water (infinite source), water_1..7 and water_full (finite volumes) + bricks
resolution1/8 block in height, 1 block across1/N block across, 1/(32 N) block in height
rateone step every 5 ticks (4 Hz)one step every tick (20 Hz, FineInterval)
volumenot conserved (sources spread, flows drain)conserved exactly, every exchange is ledgered
pressurenonecommunicating vessels through full cells

Representation#

  • Units. A sub-cell holds 0..32 units (voxel.FineCap); a block of N^3 sub-cells holds 32 N^3 units (2048 at N=4). Coarse level l (1..8) is l V units with V = 32 N^3 / 8 (256 at N=4); per sub-column a level is q = 32 N / 8 units.
  • Brick. The water of one block cell whose voxel is air: N^3 bytes, index x + N (z + N y), plus a dirty bit. Bricks live in fluid.State (map + sorted order), never in the chunks.
  • Coarse cells. water_1..water_7 hold l V units, water_full (registered only when Fine is on, after every other block, so a coarse registry keeps its ids) holds 8 V. They are finite. water stays an infinite source: it feeds neighbours up to level FineSourceLevel (default 2) and absorbs what is above that level, so a source behaves as a constant-head boundary and a sheet it feeds reaches a few blocks, not the whole world. A source with at least two source neighbours (a river, a lake, a reservoir) feeds at FineBodyLevel instead (default 6): the bank of a body holds water near the level of its surface, so a breach, a ditch or a basin fills up (sourceHead in fine_step.go). Setting it equal to FineSourceLevel turns this off.
  • Streams. The water falling from a brick over free air (see Falling streams): per lip brick, one integer queue per sub-column. Hashed, captured, snapshotted.
  • Ledger (snapshotted, not hashed: it never feeds back into the rules, so an undo returns the exact StateHash): Created (sources, pours, edits that place flowing blocks), Drained (void, sources), Evaporated (stagnant films), Displaced (edits over water). Held is the water of the bricks plus Transit, the water in the streams. Exact law, checked by the tests: coarse finite units + Held - (Created - Drained - Evaporated - Displaced) never changes.

One fine step#

Every FineInterval ticks (default 1). Everything is integer arithmetic on sorted slices; no map is iterated where order could matter (map loops only set flags or sum).

A. Demands (refine a coarse cell, spawn a brick in air or a fragile block, turn lava into stone):

  • coarse candidates (wake-ups, chunk scans, unserved demands) that satisfy the coarse predicate below;
  • every dirty brick: a face whose neighbour is air and that would spill (bottom layer wet above it; or a lateral sub-cell that is supported and holds at least the film threshold), a coarse neighbour across a seam that disagrees, lava touched by water;
  • the pressure pass: for each dirty brick touching a body of full cells (full bricks and water_full) not yet reached this step, a breadth-first walk through that body (at most FinePressure cells per step, a cut walk does nothing) collects its surfaces: non-full bricks, partial coarse cells and empty cells beside or on top of it. They are compared by head Y 32 N^3 + units; when the highest wet one and the lowest differ by more than 2 N^2 units (V when a coarse cell is involved), up to one layer (32 N^2 units) moves at once from the top of the highest to the bottom of the lowest; a coarse or empty end is refined or spawned first (a demand). This is what fills a U-tube or a basin from below.

Demands are sorted by target and served within FineSpawn per step and FineMaxBricks live bricks, starting at the hashed spawn cursor and wrapping (see Overload). A brick whose demand is refused stays dirty (it retries); a refused coarse candidate stays in the work list. A brick created or a cell converted during this phase keeps its neighbours dirty one more step (their demands were computed before the change).

B. Moves on the dirty bricks (sorted by (Y, Z, X)), sequential on live values so a pair can never overshoot:

  1. Fall, FineFallPasses passes (default 2): each sub-cell drops into the sub-cell below up to its room; the bottom layer drops into the brick below, into the void (drained) or into a source (absorbed). A source above is a spring at constant head: once per step (first pass only) it tops each top sub-cell of the brick below up to FineSourceLevel eighths of a sub-cell (8 units at the default level 2, at most 128 units per step at N=4). It used to fill the whole top layer at every pass (1024 units, half a block of water per tick), more than any lateral spreading carries away: a source placed in the air built a tower of full bricks up to itself, drawn as a stack of glassy terraces (TestFineFloatingSourceFallsAsStream).
  2. Lateral, one pass: each sub-cell equalises with its four side neighbours (inside the brick or across a face): when the giver is supported (full sub-cell or a solid / full block below) and the difference is at least 2, half the difference moves. Into a dry sub-cell only when the giver holds at least the film threshold (32 N / 16 units, 1/16 block), so a puddle stops spreading. Across a face: a source gives or absorbs toward its virtual level, the void drains. The sweep direction and the order of the four sides alternate with the step parity so no direction is favoured.
  3. Legacy evaporation (FineEvapEvery > 0 only, off by default): single-unit films with nothing above lose their unit every FineEvapEvery steps. The default sink is the evaporation of stagnant films.

C. Settle and fold. A dirty brick that neither gave nor received anything and has no refused demand becomes clean; every brick that changed makes its six neighbours dirty. A clean empty brick disappears. Each connected set of clean bricks that contains a brick that just became clean is offered to the fold:

  • each brick i of volume u_i gets level L_i = u_i / V; the remainders u_i mod V are summed, k whole levels are given to the bricks with the largest remainders (ties by cell order), and the last remainder r < V stays in residue bricks (the next in that order, as few as possible while each keeps less than the film threshold per sub-cell, so a calm film does not spread again), flattened from the bottom;
  • the result is checked against the same predicates the automaton uses (coarse predicate of every created or neighbouring coarse cell, seam and spill tests of the residue brick, support below every created cell). If any test would fire, nothing is folded (the bricks stay, clean and free, until something disturbs them).

Folding moves less than one level (V) of water per brick inside a connected calm body. The total is exact.

A calm component that the evaporation will take entirely (an isolated film in the sense below, every brick of it on a solid block) is not folded while the evaporation is on: folded, a thin puddle would become level-1 coarse cells, which nothing ever revisits. Any other component folds as before (a film larger than the budget, beside lava, the void or a stream, over air): kept in bricks without being evaporated, it would hold them forever (TestFineEvapBigFilmFolds, TestFineEvapRefusedFilmFolds).

Evaporation of stagnant films#

Small leftovers (the film a spill leaves on a lawn, a puddle after the source was removed) clear by themselves after a while instead of lingering forever. It is an explicit sink: every unit removed is added to Ledger.Evaporated, so the conservation law stays exact.

Pass. Every FineEvapPeriod ticks (default 20, one pass per second; -1 turns it off), also while the water is idle, a pass visits at most FineEvapBudget bricks (default 1024) from a cursor, in cell order, wrapping around. For each brick not classified yet in this pass it walks the brick's connected component (six faces, bricks only, at most the budget, all walks of a pass at most 4 budgets: the cursor then resumes at the first brick not visited). The component is an isolated film when it is no larger than the budget and every brick of it is:

  • a film: no water column deeper than FineEvapDepth (in 1/256 metre, default 32: 1/8 m, 16 units at N=4),
  • not the lip of a falling stream,
  • free of coarse water (a source: sea, river, spring; finite water_1..7 or water_full: a lake), lava and the void on all six faces.

A film touching anything deeper is part of that body: the shallow rim of a pool, the sheet of a spring and the shore of a sea never evaporate. Each brick of an isolated film that rests on a solid block with no water above it is tracked: since (the tick it became stagnant), at (the tick of its last evaporation), last (its units after the last visit) and rem (the remainder of the integer rate). A change of more than FineEvapEps units between two visits (default N^2, one unit per sub-column) other than its own evaporation restarts the dwell, and so does a since in the future (a malformed state; it never underflows into an immediate evaporation); a dirty brick waits. After FineEvapDwell ticks (default 500, 25 s) each visit removes floor((rate * climate * capRes * elapsed + rem) / (1000 * 1000 * 20)) units and keeps the rest in rem, so a slow rate (a cold shelter, a small FineEvapRate) still evaporates at its rate instead of rounding to nothing. capRes is the volume of a film at the threshold depth, elapsed the ticks since the brick's last evaporation (or the end of its dwell), at most 10 periods (a long rain or dirty spell does not come back as one large step), rate the permille per second FineEvapRate (default 30: a film at the threshold depth clears in about 33 s, a thinner one sooner) and climate the permille factor:

conditionfactor
rain or snow on an open brick (weather extra, local phase)0
sheltered: a solid or opaque block within 64 cells above (a roof, a cave)x0.25
open: sun height (weather day clock)x0.5 at night .. x1.5 at noon
temperature at the brick (weather.Local.TempMilliC)x0.1 at -5 C and below, x1 at 15 C, x1.5 at 25 C and above
no weather extra installed, or the module installed but off (every game installs it, inert unless the project enables it)x1 (open), x0.25 (sheltered)

The units are taken from the deepest columns first, one unit per column and round from its top wet sub-cell, so the film thins evenly; the brick and its neighbours are made dirty (the step settles them, an emptied brick disappears). The mesh draws the depth per corner (below), so the surface and its film look fade out with the volume, without a step.

State and determinism. The since/at/last/rem of the tracked bricks and the cursor are hashed (appended to the brick digest only when something is tracked or the cursor has moved, so a state without films keeps its former hash), captured, snapshotted (fine.evap, validated) and restored; a world file (PrepareFineBytes) drops them like the dirty bits. Every input of a pass is hashed state: the tick, the bricks, the voxels and the weather extra. A Config with an evaporation field set hashes them ('E'). The rules changed without changing the default Config, so a fine Config also hashes the rules version fluid.FineRules (1 = with evaporation, 2 = overload, 3 = tall falls, 4 = body head, 5 = water under a body of water): a journal recorded under the former rules fails its first hash check at replay instead of diverging later, a snapshot written by a newer rules version is refused with an explicit error, and an older snapshot (a save) restores and continues under the current rules. Stats.FineEvapVisited is the bricks examined by the last pass.

An edit that removes a brick (fluid_cell with an empty fill, a block placed over it) drops its tracking like its dirty bit; re-creating it (the editor's undo through fluid_cell) starts a new dwell, so that undo, like the dirty bit it restores as dirty, does not return the exact StateHash of a state with a tracked film. Chunk-mode undo and rollbacks capture the whole extra and do return it.

Cost. One pass per FineEvapPeriod: at most FineEvapBudget visits and 4 FineEvapBudget bricks walked (six map reads each), one column scan of at most 64 blocks per evaporating brick. 4096 droplets on a 128x128 map (TestFineEvapBoundedCost, budget 256) clear without a pass examining more than the budget.

Coarse water. The coarse automaton (W1) needs no equivalent: an unfed flowing cell, a level-1 puddle included, already drains (one cell per step from its edge). In fine mode, level-1 coarse cells that were folded before this rule existed (an older save) are left as they are.

Tests (fluid/fine_evap_test.go): a puddle clears between its dwell and the expected time, the ledger exact at every tick; a lake of finite coarse cells, a pit of poured bricks and a river of sources keep every unit while a puddle beside them clears; rain stops the open puddle while a roofed one dries, and the open one clears after the rain; snapshot, restore, journal replay and same-hash-same-future while films are tracked (the dwell is in the hash); bounded passes on 4096 films; random edits with a fast evaporation keep the conservation law at every tick.

Falling streams#

Before streams, a cascade was a column of bricks, one per block crossed: the fall rule moves a sub-cell down by at most FineFallPasses sub-cells per step, so the falling water sat as a thin packet (about 6 of 32 units) in one layer of each cell, and the renderer had to invent a curtain between the packets (notches where a cell had no brick at that tick). Water in free fall now has its own continuous representation.

Lip. A dirty brick whose bottom layer is wet, whose cell below is air without a brick, and whose column has at least FineStreamMinFall (default 2) free cells under it. Its stream is created in phase A (demands, canonical order: dirty bricks sorted by cell; within FineMaxStreams, default 1024) instead of the spill demand that would spawn the brick below. A drop of one cell keeps the old rule (the brick below is spawned).

State (per lip, sorted by lip cell): q, D slots of N^2 integers (units per sub-column), slot 0 arriving at the next step, slot D-1 the water that just left. Hashed (lip, D, every slot) after the bricks, only when a stream exists (a state without falling water keeps its former hash); captured (deep copy), snapshotted (fine.streams, validated: sorted, in bounds, 1 <= D <= 181, slots >= 0, not empty, at most the cap), restored. The impact cell of the last step is carried for the renderer but neither hashed nor read by the rules.

Step (phase B, after the fall and lateral passes of the dirty bricks):

  1. Leave the lip. In the fall passes, a bottom sub-cell of a lip that feeds its stream moves entirely into the stream's input of its sub-column (no room limit: free fall). The lip is "moved", so it stays dirty while it feeds.
  2. Walk. From the lip down through air cells without brick, at most 256 cells (64 before rules 3): the target is the first brick that is not full; a void or a source absorbs (drained, like the fall rule); a fragile block is the target itself (spawned, the block destroyed); anything else (wall, coarse water, lava, a full brick) puts the target in the free cell above it. With nothing free under the lip (h = 0) that cell is the lip itself, which an edit may have turned into anything: a lip brick or air is the target as usual, a source or a void absorbs, any other block (stone, lava, coarse flowing water...) blocks the stream: what arrives is ledgered as Displaced (like an edit over water), so the stream empties and the automaton can go idle (TestStreamBlockedLipDrains). The fall height h is the number of free cells crossed. Everything is read from the hashed state of this step.
  3. Resize to the delay D (delayOf): free fall at 32 blocks/s^2 up to a terminal speed of 30 blocks/s (reached after 14 blocks and 0.94 s), 20 steps/s, rounded up, 1 for h = 0: ceil(5 sqrt(h)) for h <= 14, ceil(20 (64 h + 900) / 1920) above (150 blocks: 110 steps, 5.5 s; 256 blocks: 181). A shorter fall merges the slots beyond the new length into slot 0 (that water arrives now), a longer one appends empty slots (the water in the air keeps its schedule, the new water travels further).
  4. Deposit slot 0 into the target brick: each sub-column fills its own sub-column from the bottom, then the rest of the brick layer by layer; what does not fit, or what waits for a brick to be spawned, stays in slot 0.
  5. Shift: slot k takes slot k+1, the input enters slot D-1. A stream whose total is 0 is removed.

A target that is free air with nothing below it in range asks for a brick in phase A of the next step (a demand served like the others, within FineSpawn and FineMaxBricks; a refused one is simply asked again: the stream keeps the automaton busy). A stream that exists makes Idle false.

Back-pressure. A lip feeds its stream only while slot 0 (the water waiting at the target) holds at most one brick of water (N^3 FineCap units); past it the lip follows the normal rules (it asks for the brick below, starved when the cap refuses it), so a target that accepts nothing (brick cap, spawn budget, a full pool) never makes slot 0 grow without bound. One step moves at most FineFallPasses full sub-cells per sub-column into a stream, so every slot value of a reachable state is at most N^3 FineCap + 181 FineFallPasses FineCap (streamSlotMax, 13632 at N=4), which is also the bound the snapshot parser enforces: every reachable state is restorable (TestStreamBackPressureAtBrickCap, TestStreamBackPressureSourceAboveHoles, TestStreamSlotBoundRestorable). A blob with any stream is rejected when streams are off (FineMaxStreams: -1).

Interruptions. A block placed in the fall, a lip that dries up or is removed, a basin that fills: the walk of the next step sees it (the water already in the air lands on the block, or arrives at the shorter delay, or keeps falling without new input until the queue is empty). Nothing in a stream can refer to a stale cell: the lip is only a key, the target is recomputed every step.

Determinism. Streams are created and walked in sorted order, the deposit order is the stream order, and every input is a function of the hashed state, so two states with the same StateHash have the same future (TestStreamSameHashSameFuture, the restored engine with every brick dirty included). Undo/redo in chunk mode and the rollback of a failed act capture the whole fluid extra, streams included (agent: TestFineWaterStreamsChunkModeUndoAndReplay, TestFineWaterStreamsActRollback).

World files. A .voxworld holds voxels and bricks, not the water in the air: PrepareFineBytes ledgers the transit as displaced and clears the streams (a load replaces the water like the voxels; TestFineStreamsLoadWorldMidFallReplays loads a world saved mid-fall into editors holding other water and checks the journal replay and the hash, three loads).

Bounds. At most FineMaxStreams streams (-1 turns them off: the fall goes through bricks as before), each at most 181 slots of N^2 integers (11.3 KiB at N=4); a step walks at most 256 cells per stream twice (demands and advance). A 500-block fall (TestStreamVeryTallFallBounded) is two chained streams and costs a few milliseconds per step at most.

Tall falls (rules version 3). Before, a stream walked 64 cells: a 150-block fall was three streams, each landing in a brick hanging in mid-air and restarting from rest there (the water reached the foot after 108 ticks instead of the 61 of a free fall, with a splash ring and a thin filet in the sky every 64 blocks), and the renderer drew the whole curtain, lip to impact, at the first tick of a new stream (the water seemed to arrive at once). Now one stream covers 256 cells with a terminal speed, and the export carries FrontGap / TailGap (voxel.FineStream: the part of the fall below the oldest water in the air, above the newest one; both 0 for a steady stream): the curtain grows from the lip with its front (white tip, no splash at the foot before the water arrives) and a dried lip leaves a falling tail. The streamer re-meshes a chunk when the front or the tail moves by 1/64 of the fall, and meshes the chunk layers above the generated terrain that hold water but no edited block (the air beside a tower: the curtain had a gap in each). The curtain vertices carry the time since the lip (1/28 s, curtainTau) instead of the fall in 1/4 block, which saturated 64 blocks down and froze the streaks into stretched lines; the shader advects the streaks with that time, so they accelerate, then run at the terminal speed.

Tests (fluid/fine_stream_test.go): formation and exact conservation at every tick, a cascade without bricks in the air (and the same scene with streams off peaks higher in bricks), monotone delays, cut by a block / re-formed / dried up with conservation, snapshot mid-fall (other engine, same engine, transit in the hash), rollback of a failed tick, same hash same future, random edits in and around the fall (blocks, pours, the source toggled; two engines agree, law at every tick), the cap, the wire validation.

Water under a body of water#

A source with a head above FineSourceLevel (a sea, a lake: see sourceHead) is the bottom of a body of water, so a cell opened under its surface must end full, not with a surface of its own (rules version 5):

  • Submerged source. A body source with a source above it (below the surface) feeds its side neighbours at a full block (sourceHead returns at least 8): a tunnel dug sideways from the sea, a breach low in a bank. A breach onto open ground floods a larger sheet than at the body level (TestFineSourceAndSeaLedger: ~1150 bricks, rest at ~63000 ticks, against ~600 and ~29000).
  • Fall. The source above a brick tops each top sub-cell up to full, not to FineSourceLevel, once the sub-cell under it is full (the water can fall no further). A floating source never qualifies: its water falls.
  • Lateral. A brick is submerged when the column above it is bricks and full coarse cells up to a source (submergedAt, at most 64 cells, read from the topology only); a body source beside a submerged brick feeds the whole layer.
  • Under a ceiling. A brick whose cell above is a wall, a full cell or a source, joined through bricks and full coarse cells that are all under a ceiling to a submerged source beside one of them (confinedUnder, at most 64 cells, breadth first), has its top sub-cells topped up to full once they rest on full ones: between two bricks water only moves for a difference of 2 units, so a tunnel fed from its mouth rested with a slope of one unit per sub-cell (92 % full at its far end). A closed tank with no source keeps its volume.
  • Pressure. A body source on top of a body of full cells, or a submerged source beside it, is a front of that body: an inexhaustible giver at its head that gives the whole difference (not half), so a closed cave under the sea floor, opened by one block, floods to its ceiling.
  • Coarse predicate and fold. A full coarse cell beside a body source is at rest when the cell is submerged (calmBeside): it neither refines nor stops a calm full brick there from folding (a block broken in the sea used to stay three bricks for ever).

Before, a block dug under the sea floor kept 6.5/8 of water forever, with a visible surface inside the sea, and a pillar broken in the sea never rested (the source above poured, the sources beside absorbed). Tests in fluid/fine_submerged_test.go: TestDugUnderTheSeaFillsUp, TestBrokenInTheSeaFillsUp, TestCaveUnderTheSeaFloods, TestTunnelFromTheSeaFillsUp (each settles, ends full, folds back into blocks, and keeps the volume law at every tick).

Voxel size#

A world with smaller voxels (sim.Engine.Subdiv() = 2, 4 or 8 voxels per metre) keeps the brick: N sub-cells per voxel axis, so the resolution per metre is N*Subdiv (finer, Subdiv^3 times more bricks per cubic metre; the user accepts the memory). The rules read the voxel size every tick (Config.atScale, a working copy that is never hashed), so a scene switch to another size takes effect at once, with no registry change (the fine water keeps scale 1 blocks):

  • Speed. The tick runs Subdiv fine steps (the whole step: spills, falls, sideways flow, streams, folds), so the front and the falls cover the same metres per second. The evaporation pass belongs to the tick. Subdiv 1 is one step.
  • Spread. The heads of a spring and of a body (FineSourceLevel, FineBodyLevel) are depths in metres: eighths of a metre at scale 1, so Subdiv times more eighths of a voxel, no longer capped at one voxel (8). The part above the top voxel of a source column is served by the virtual head (virtualHead, headRises): the head is measured from the bottom source of the column, and an air cell beside a brick, reached from that column through air only, is a virtual source at the level the head still reaches in that layer (head - 8 * layers above the bottom source); a brick whose top layer is three quarters full against such a cell spawns the brick above it (water never spills upward). A spring is thus 25 cm deep at every voxel size (two voxels at Subdiv 8) and a deep lake adds nothing (its head is within its own voxels). A source belongs to a body of water when two of its side neighbours one metre away are sources (a one metre spring is no lake). The film threshold grows with Subdiv, capped at a half sub-cell up to Subdiv 2, 3/8 at 4 and 1/4 at 8 (filmFor): the finer sheet spreads by smaller steps and a thinner film keeps its reach in metres.
  • Caps. FineMaxBricks, FinePressure, the evaporation budget and FineBurstBricks grow by Subdiv^2 (the sheet's area), the bricks (cap plus burst) up to 64 MiB of sub-cells; FineSpawn by Subdiv. The burst step stays an eighth of the scaled range. FineInterval and FineFallPasses are unchanged. The overload fold and the forced fold run every overFoldEvery / overForceEvery ticks (the step counter advances Subdiv times per tick).
  • Metres. FineEvapDepth is a depth in 1/256 metre: the same metres at every size (Subdiv times the voxel fraction, up to 3/4 of a voxel so a full brick is never a film). Plugin.FocusRadius is in metres (radius * Subdiv voxels, within MaxFocusRadius) and its snap is 8 metres; fluid_focus itself takes voxels.
  • Mesh. mesh/finewater.go works in voxel units, like the coarse mesh: the 8/9 lowering of a free surface and the seams with coarse cells are fractions of the voxel, and the sheet's slope per sub-column does not depend on the size, so seams stay consistent (TestFineSeamWithCoarseCell).
  • Tests: TestFineSubdivSpreadMetres (a one metre spring spreads within 0.75 m of the scale 1 front at 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s and 0.5 m at 10 s, at Subdiv 2, 4 and 8), TestSpringHeadDeeperThanAVoxel, TestScaledCapsInMetres, TestEvapDepthInMetres, TestOverFoldPeriodInTicks, TestFocusSnapInMetres, TestFineSubdivSeamWithSource, TestFineSubdivBricksAndVolume, TestFineSubdivChangeAtRuntime, TestFineLakeSurfaceMetres (a 3 m x 3 m basin filled from a 1 m spring settles, at Subdiv 8 and at scale 1, to the same depth within 0.035 m, one sub-cell of a brick at 12.5 cm voxels; measured at the far corner and the far edge, not beside the spring where a scale 1 cell averages a metre of the head gradient; the depth is the evaporation film depth, 1/8 m, plus the spring's reach).
  • Waking. An edit of a source wakes the bricks that read its head from up to ceil(head / 8) layers above it (dirtyBody, State.headLayers; one layer at scale 1), because the virtual head of a brick beside a column of air is measured from a source several voxels below: a removed spring drains the second layer too (TestFineRemovedSpringDrains, TestFineSourceEditWakesTheVirtualHeadLayers). voxel.Access (what sim.Chunks hands to systems such as the physics character) exposes the fine water like the map (FineSource), so a character's immersion sees the bricks.

Opposing flows#

Sources whose water meets (a channel fed from both ends, springs facing each other, channels crossing, a loop, a spring against a finite coarse lake or lava) settle without oscillating: no state of the bricks comes back two steps later, and each scene of TestFineOpposingFlowsSettle goes idle (channel 235 ticks, head on 800, crossing 210, loop 323, lake 1899, lava 312) with the volume exact at every tick. A symmetric layout settles to the same water on both sides within one level (the fold gives its whole levels to the largest remainders, ties by cell order) or 2% (the lateral pass is sequential; its direction alternates every step, which leaves a bias of about 1%).

Push. The current that pushes a body (voxel.LiquidTable.Flow: the controller's water push, Immersion.Flow) was a unit vector toward any lower neighbour: a film a few units higher than its neighbour pushed at full speed, and where two flows met the push flipped from full speed one way to full speed the other way every few ticks (25 flips in 300 ticks, amplitude 0.69 at the meeting line of TestFineOpposingPushNoJitter). It now grows with the head difference up to full strength at one coarse level, so coarse water (whose differences are whole levels) pushes exactly as before and the meeting line of fine water pushes with an amplitude under 0.02. The water surface's current (mesh flowOf) was already proportional and clamped: no flipped or NaN direction where flows cancel.

Rules shared by coarse and fine cells (hysteresis)#

A coarse cell only knows its level, so the coarse and seam tests use a tolerance of one level; this is what makes a fold stable instead of refining again:

testfires when
coarse predicate, cell of level Lair, a fragile block or the void below; a partial coarse cell or a source below; liquid above a partial cell; a side neighbour that is air / void with L >= 2, a coarse cell with abs(L - L') >= 2, a source with abs(L - S) >= 2, lava (when it can turn to stone)
source predicateair or a fragile block below or beside it, lava beside it
seam, brick face column a (units) vs coarse level Labs(a - L q) > q; coarse partial below a wet bottom layer; coarse water above a top layer that has room
spill into airbottom layer wet (down); a supported sub-cell with at least the film threshold (sides)

Bricks are invisible to the coarse predicate (a coarse cell next to a brick is the brick's business). The thresholds are the same seen from both sides: a clean brick never has a coarse neighbour whose test would fire, so removing it or folding next to it cannot leave an unstable coarse cell that nobody woke.

Waking and determinism#

  • Dirty bits are state: they are hashed, captured, snapshotted. Every voxel write (voxel.Observer.BlockChanged) marks the bricks of the 15 cells it can influence dirty synchronously; ChunkReplaced (world loads, agent history, undo in chunk mode) marks every brick of the chunk and its one-cell border dirty, removes the bricks whose cell is no longer air (ledgered as displaced) and ledgers the change of coarse volume of the chunk; fluid_wake marks the bricks of its box. Writes commute (a flag, a sum), so the arrival order of a command's writes cannot matter.
  • The chunk scans still to do (queue, sweep and its cursor) are hashed in fine mode, and a wake-up inside a chunk that is still to be scanned waits for that scan. The rest of the coarse work list (pending writes, unserved demands) works as in W1 (sorted multisets; past 8192 writes in one step the writes become the sorted set of their chunks; past 4096 queued chunks a sweep of the overlay chunks that also looks at the six neighbours of each chunk, base terrain included). It is not hashed: it is a superset of the coarse cells whose predicate fires outside the chunks still to scan, so two states with the same hash evaluate the same demands. Candidates are evaluated in cell order until 4 FineSpawn of them fired; a stale entry costs one predicate and neither budget nor a hit, and the rest is carried over untouched. Only the evaluated, refused candidates count against MaxActive and the hashed Dropped; the carried ones are bounded separately (MaxActive plus one step of wake-ups and scans) and an excess, which needs far more unstable cells than the spawn budget serves for a long time, is shed without being hashed (Stats.CarryShed).
  • Every other choice (sweep direction, side order) depends on the tick, which is hashed. Hence: same StateHash, same future, including after Snapshot/Restore in the middle of a flow, a failed tick's rollback, a chunk-mode undo (agent/hist.go captures the whole extra) and VerifyReplay after a world load.
  • Assumption shared with W1: water of the base terrain that is unstable but never touched is not evaluated (the world generator only produces stable sea).

Overload#

Many sources (or one large flood) can ask for more bricks than FineMaxBricks. Before rules version 2 this was a cliff:

  • Deadlock. A brick whose spill the cap refused stayed dirty, and a connected set of bricks folds only when none of them is dirty. The waiting front of a sheet therefore kept the whole sheet in bricks, nothing ever folded, the pool never emptied: the water froze half spread, with straight cut edges drawn as skirts, dry ground between the sources, and a world that was never idle (100 sources on a plane: 2048 bricks and 1248 dirty bricks forever, 3.7 ms per tick for nothing; at 50 sources, 174 waiting bricks held 1874 calm ones).
  • Starvation. The demands were served from the lowest target every step, so whatever room the budgets left went to the same cells again: the sources with the highest coordinates waited for every lower front.

Rules version 2 (fluid.FineRules = 2):

  1. Fair service. The demands of a step are served in target order from the hashed spawn cursor, wrapping; when a budget (FineSpawn or the cap) refuses one, the cursor moves to it, so the next step starts where this one stopped. A pending demand is served within about pending / FineSpawn steps of room. The cursor is hashed (appended to the brick digest only while it is set: a state where every demand is served keeps its former hash), captured, snapshotted (fine.spawn, validated) and dropped by a world file like the dirty bits.
  2. Overload fold. On a step whose cap refused a demand, every 4 steps, the clean bricks next to a brick that is held (dirty only because its demand was refused: it neither gave nor received) are offered to the fold without it: the calm water behind a waiting front becomes coarse cells with the same stability tests as any fold, plus no seam with the held brick (a residue brick may touch it). The freed bricks go to the waiting fronts.
  3. Forced fold. When that frees nothing (a pool made only of fronts and thin films that cannot fold alone), every 20 steps whole components whose dirty bricks are all held are folded with the lateral tests waived, in cell order from the spawn cursor, until 1/8 of the cap is free. Vertical tests stay (support, water above a partial cell), so no coarse water hangs in the air; a front cell becomes a coarse cell whose predicate fires, which asks for a brick again when there is room. A component with a brick that moved is never forced.

Every fold moves less than one level per brick and is exact: the conservation law holds at every tick of every overload test. The water keeps spreading at the rate the pool allows and the scene settles to the same coarse water as with an unlimited pool, later.

Signal. Stats.FineOverload (the last step's cap refused a demand), FineCapRefused, FineCapSources (of which raised by a source), FineCapSteps, FineOverFolds, FineOverFolded; State.Overloaded() and State.OverloadNote() ("water overloaded: 2048/2048 bricks, 312 spills waiting (41 sources throttled)"). The fluid.Plugin writes one line to standard error when the water becomes overloaded and one when it recovers (held 100 ticks, at most one line per 200 ticks; Log redirects, Quiet silences) and, with OverloadHUD, adds the note to the HUD lines (the sandbox turns it on).

Cost of a step. The move phases (fall passes, lateral pass) run on several goroutines (fluid.Threads, default GOMAXPROCS up to 6; 1 is sequential) and give exactly the water of the sequential order: each brick gets a level one above the highest level of the earlier bricks it conflicts with (the brick and its four side bricks are written, the bricks under them are read), and the bricks of a level, which touch no common brick, run together. The StateHash does not depend on the number of threads (TestFineThreadsDoNotChangeTheWater, also run under -race). The bricks also keep pointers to their six neighbours (no map lookup to resolve them) and the pressure walk follows them. Together: 1.8x on the 1000-source plane and cave scenes (11.9 and 21.5 ms per tick at FineMaxBricks 16384, was 21.1 and 39.7), identical results. A brick costs about 1 to 1.5 us per step now, so FineMaxBricks 8192 to 16384 fits a 20 Hz budget on a desktop (FineMaxBricks * N^3 <= 2^24 still holds); the default stays 4096 because the config is hashed.

Adaptive cap. Config.FineBurstBricks (default 0: off) lets the cap on the live bricks follow the load: the effective cap (State.FineCap, Stats.FineCap) is FineMaxBricks + burst. On every step where the cap refused a demand the burst grows by an eighth of FineBurstBricks (up to it); after 100 calm steps (nothing refused, also at rest) it shrinks by the same amount once the bricks fit under the lower cap. FineMaxBricks + FineBurstBricks is the memory and cost ceiling (same limits as FineMaxBricks). The burst and its calm counter are state: hashed while the burst is above 0 (a state at its base cap keeps its former hash), written in snapshots (fine.burst, validated against the config) and captured for undo; the config field is hashed only when set. It is a function of the steps, not of the clock, so every engine agrees (TestFineBurstIsState, TestFineBurstGrowsAndShrinks). 1000-source plane, 4096 + 12288: every source served at tick 67 instead of 383, 12.4 ms per tick at the 16384 peak instead of 4.4 ms at the fixed 4096; the cave needs more than the ceiling and stays overloaded, at 16.5 ms per tick. Combine it with the focus: the focus bounds what needs bricks, the burst gives that part the room it needs for as long as it needs it.

Focus. fluid_focus {points, radius} (or fluid.Plugin{FocusRadius: 48}, which follows the camera and FocusPoints) limits the fine step to the bricks whose column is within radius voxels of a point (the plugin's FocusRadius is in metres). Outside the focus the water is frozen like an unloaded chunk: its bricks keep their dirty bits and are not evaluated, nothing spawns there (a demand outside waits; a brick inside forgets it and is woken when the focus reaches the target), calm bricks outside fold back to coarse cells (64 offered per step, in a window that rotates with the tick) and the engine goes idle when nothing inside the focus moves. A brick inside may still push water into a brick across the border. The focus (radius and sorted points) is hashed (only while set: no focus keeps the former hash), snapshotted, captured for undo and journaled; the points are sorted, so their order is not state. Widening or clearing the focus resumes the frozen water, which settles to the same coarse water as without a focus (TestFineFocusFreezesOutsideAndResumes). Measured on the 1000-source scenes (default caps, 1000 ticks): plane, no focus: cap reached, never idle; radius 24: 2.7 ms per tick, no overload, idle at tick 376. Cave: 7.7 ms per tick, cap reached; radius 24: 4.6 ms, cap not reached.

Compatibility#

  • Config.Fine is off by default: the coarse automaton, the registry (no water_full), StateHash and every golden are unchanged (TestFineOffIsTheCoarseAutomaton, the scenario goldens).
  • In a project, runtime.plugins.fine_water is on by default and only read when runtime.plugins.fluid is true; "fine_water": false selects the coarse automaton. The editor without a project (burrowedit, -no-fine-water to turn it off) and burrowd sessions created with fine_water: true follow the same rule. Saves taken with the coarse water do not load with the fine water. burrowedit -project follows the project, so the editor and burrowplay simulate the same water.
  • A .voxworld with bricks stores them after the chunks; a coarse engine refuses such a file with an explicit error.

For S3 (renderer)#

mesh.BuildFineWater(grid, reader, liquidTable, chunk, opts) (or mesh.Opts.FineWater on the mesher, which fills Result.Fine) builds the surface of the bricks of one chunk from an immutable voxel.FineGrid (State.ExportFine(lo, hi), safe to hand to a mesher goroutine). The coarse faces are unchanged; the fine mesh is an extra indexed triangle list (quads of 4 vertices, 6 indices, counter-clockwise seen from outside) drawn in the water pass:

mesh.FineVertex fieldtypemeaning
Pos[3]float32chunk-local position in blocks (fractional: the surface moves in 1/(32 N) block)
Normal[3]int8unit normal x127 (central differences of the corner heights for the top)
Flow[2]int8surface current (x, z) x127, from the column volumes, downhill
Depthuint8water depth of the column in 1/16 block (capped)
Foamuint8foam potential 0..255: speed, plus skirts (falling or spilling water) and undersides

Blockvoxel.BlockID (uint16)the water block, for its texture layer and tint

24 bytes in Go (packed: 22). The coarse vertex (mesh.Vertex, integer positions) cannot hold fractional heights, so S3 needs a second vertex layout and pipeline sharing the water fragment shader. Seams: on a block corner that touches a coarse liquid cell the height equals LiquidCornerHeights of the coarse neighbours (LiquidTable.BlockCornerHeight: the mean height of the liquid cells around the corner, bricks only when a brick touches it, see below), along a block edge shared with a coarse cell it is the straight line between the two corners, so the fine surface meets the coarse quads without cracks (TestFineSeamWithCoarseCell, TestFineSurfaceHasNoCracks). Elsewhere the surface is built from sheets (next section). Opts.FineMaxVertices (default 65536) truncates a pathological chunk and sets FineMesh.Truncated.

Rendering (S3, done)#

The HDR shader levels draw it: the streamer exports the bricks and the streams once per simulation tick (State.ExportFine, found by duck typing on the chunk map's fine source; FineGrid.Streams), and re-meshes only the water of the chunks whose bricks (or one-cell border) or streams changed (Mesher.BuildWater, on the mesh workers, at most every 2 ticks per chunk, never while a build of that chunk is queued). The renderer swaps the chunk's water buffer alone (Renderer.UploadChunkWaterOnly) and keeps drawing the old one until the new one is uploaded: no cube re-mesh, no gap. Water meshes are joined with the coarse liquid surface (mesh.Opts.LiquidSurface, same FineVertex layout) and drawn by water2.vert + water_hdr.frag.

Cascades (mesh/cascade.go; all frame domain, nothing written to the simulation):

  • Curtain (FineCurtain): a ribbon from the lip surface (the height of the feeding neighbour, so the sheet continues the water above the edge, no lump) to the impact surface found by walking the voxels and bricks. It leaves the lip with a horizontal tangent along the flow over the edge and follows the free-fall parabola (horizontal speed from the debit: thick water shoots further), then drops. Its width covers the wet sub-columns; ribbons of neighbouring lips with the same direction join exactly (shared boundary vertices: thickness, lip and impact heights averaged at the boundary, the same row fractions for every ribbon), open ends taper (alpha to 0 over 0.22 block, wobbling in the vertex shader), and a thin filet narrows as it accelerates. Shader: thickness = debit (Depth), streaks advected along sqrt(fall) so they accelerate downward, wide "panels" across, aeration growing with debit and fall (thin = glassy, refracting the cliff behind through the scene copy with a sky reflection and a sun glint; thick = white, lit through), a white foot over the last block.
  • Foot: spray droplets (FineSpray, at most 20 per lip, ballistic loops animated on the GPU from a per-vertex seed), churning boils and a faint rising mist (FineMist, 2..6 + 1..3 sprites per lip), expanding foam rings and boils on the water (FineRing, a flat quad, radius from the energy). On the levels without HDR these are dropped in the vertex shader. The spray and mist sprites are soft: they fade over a short height above the impact level and a short depth in front of the opaque scene, and the curtain fades into the pool over its last half block, so the depth test does not cut a hard white line (a fake ledge) along the foot.
  • Foam on the surface (water_hdr.frag): the current foam pattern is advected with a two-phase periodic drift (flow-map technique), not by flow * time, which sheared the pattern without bound where the flow varies (moire on the small basins under a rill). Shore foam uses the distance to the opaque scene (max(vertical depth, path / 2)), so a wall seen at a low angle gets a thin soft fringe instead of a wide band.
  • The lip bricks are not drawn (their water is the head of the curtain) while they hold only falling water: at most half full and no wet bottom sub-column resting on a block, coarse water or a full sub-cell below (a lip whose fall was blocked keeps its puddle, TestCascadeSupportedLipIsDrawn). A chunk resolves only the streams of its column and its one-cell border (TestCascadeFarChunkSkipsStreams); the streamer re-meshes the neighbouring column of a lip on a chunk border (joined curtains) and keeps exporting a grid that has streams but no brick left (engine/TestSyncFineStreams). The former stream heuristics of the brick mesh (packets joined into a sheet, gaps filled) remain for drops of one cell and for streams over the cap.
  • Sheets (mesh/finewater.go, clusterAt): a sub-column draws a top quad only where its water is supported (every layer under its top wet, the bottom one on a solid block, coarse water or a full sub-cell) and uncovered. Each such surface, each coarse surface and each dry floor ("ground") of the four columns around a lattice point, in the rows floor(h)-2 .. floor(h)+2, is a candidate; sorted by global height, candidates less than 0.55 block apart form one sheet, and the corner takes the mean of its sheet. A sheet spans at most three gaps, never the ends of the window, so every column of the sheet, whatever its row, gets the same corner (no crack, TestFineSurfaceHasNoCracks): a mound or a slope over a step is one smooth surface across cell rows instead of terraces with vertical faces between them (TestFineSheetAcrossRows). A film next to dry ground averages with it and tapers to 0.015 above it: no skirt, no stair outline (TestFineFilmTapersToGround). A skirt is drawn only toward a neighbour that is not part of the sheet, from the corner heights down to the neighbour's lower sheet or the bottom of the water column (it can span several rows), and only when it is at least 0.07 block tall (a thinner one read as a blue line). Water that rests on nothing (a packet between two ticks) never enters a sheet: the stream heuristics below draw it, and a packet of less than one sub-cell in mid-air (a film spilling over a one-block step) is not drawn at all. Before, every corner averaged the columns of its own row: a falling packet or a covered column (height 1) next to a puddle pulled the corner up into spikes, and a partly filled brick pinned to its mean height at the block corners drew a sawtooth.
  • Coarse/brick corners: LiquidTable.SurfaceCorners averages only the bricks at a block corner that a brick touches. A coarse source is drawn full but holds its fine neighbours at its low constant head (FineSourceLevel), so the old mean pinned the brick surface to a steep strip along the seam (a notched band seen from the side); the coarse cell now slopes down to the bricks over its own width.
  • Shading of thin and standing water (water_hdr.frag): the foam of the current and of the shore needs a body of water (it fades out under ~0.2 block of column depth: a film is wet ground, not a field of white blotches); the current is the head difference across a column (flowOf, a quarter block over two sub-columns is full speed), not the raw unit difference that made every film a torrent; a film reflects less and darkens the ground a little. A side face of standing water (vertex foam under 70) gets no falling streaks, and its refraction path is capped by the column depth (it crossed everything behind the face: blue slivers). The path under any surface is capped at three column depths. A steep slope seen from its back (water running over a ledge) is shaded as a surface from above with its normal turned toward the eye, not with the underwater colour (a dark blue band along every ledge).
  • White water (curtain aeration, spray, mist, boils, rings): lit by the sky dome by day (the terrain's hemisphere term alone left it darker than the sky-reflecting pool: a grey, dirty band at the foot) and at half intensity under the moon (it glowed far above the moonlit ground); moonlight caustics are faint. The curtain's glassy part has more water body (absorption over 0.3 + 0.7 thickness block, stronger in-scatter) and brighter streaks. Each foam ring has its own phase from its centre and the boil is a world-space pattern on a soft white base, so the foot of a wide fall is one band of churning water instead of a chain of identical circles with dark holes.
  • Depth per corner: the Depth of a top vertex is the sheet height at that lattice point over the bottom of its column (rounded to 1/16 block), not one value per column: over a flat floor the corner height is shared, so the depth (and every term driven by it: foam, the film look) varies continuously instead of in sub-column squares. Across a step in the floor the two columns still disagree at the shared corner. The attribute keeps its 1/16 block unit (shared with the coarse surface): a drying film loses its depth-driven look in 1/16 steps, while its surface height goes down continuously.
  • Shallow water at noon (water_hdr.frag): the caustics gain is 1 + 1.2 c / (1 + 0.4 c) (at most x2.8; it was 1 + 1.4 c, up to x6 where two veins cross, which clipped a bright sand or grass bottom to white in shallow water and bloom smeared it); the shore foam needs about 0.3 block of water (in a pool a few tenths deep every pixel was within the shore distance of the bottom: white blotches over the whole pool), and so does the current foam (every shallow flowing film, and the calm finite water_1..7 sheets that the coarse surface marks with a flowing foam potential, was covered with white patches); the foam rings of a fall's foot fade out over less than half a block of water (a flat quad crossing a stepped film showed through as stepped white patches). Every pow with a base that can be negative or out of range is clamped or written as a product (pow(x, 2.0) of a negative x in the rings returned NaN on NVIDIA, which bloom spread into a white smear) and the normals are normalised with a zero-length fallback.
  • Merging: a brick whose N^2 top quads are coplanar (calm, flat, no skirt) is emitted as one quad keeping the attributes of its four corners; the per-corner current and foam of the sub-column quads are the mean of the columns around each corner (no flat square of foam per sub-column).
  • LOD: beyond 96 blocks (horizontal distance to the chunk centre) the bricks are drawn by the coarse surface, one quad per cell at the brick's mean height (Opts.FineLOD); curtains are kept. Crossing the distance re-meshes the water.

Reading: the cascade's look costs about +0.2 ms in the water half (curtain, spray, mist, rings); every scene stays under the budget (medium <= 6 ms, ultra <= 10 ms of GPU at 1440p, radius 12). The run-to-run noise of these GPU numbers is about +-0.4 ms (the v2 river, untouched, moved by +0.45 ms at medium). The streams halve the bricks of the cascade (178 -> 80) and of the sheet (641 -> 326: no brick column under the ledge), which divides the sheet's fluid tick by 6.8 (9.4 -> 1.4 ms, worst 15 -> 1.6 ms) and its GPU water by 1.6. The mesh workers no longer rebuild the cube mesh for moving water: the worst build drops from 5.7 to 2.7 ms (waterfall) and from 45-82 to 3.7-5.0 ms (sheet); the sheet's worker time per frame drops from 26-49 ms to 7-8 ms although its water re-meshes twice as often. Nothing of this runs on the main thread (frame CPU 0.6-0.9 ms in every row).

Simulation alone, A/B with the same build (go test ./fluid -run TestFineCostReport -v, fast sizes, N=4): cascade 0.042 ms/tick and 15 bricks with streams, 0.054 ms/tick and 21 bricks with FineMaxStreams: -1; the other reference scenes have no fall of two cells or more and are unchanged.

Water mesh of the waterfall's chunks (a temporary benchmark of Mesher.BuildWater on the three chunks of the scene after 200 ticks): 5.4 ms -> 4.2 ms with the 27-chunk read cache -> 2.8 ms reading the chunk's own voxels from the mesher's copy.

Not done: a faster fold of calm bricks and skipping stable bricks beyond what the dirty bits already do (every clean brick is already skipped; the sheet stays expensive because a constant-head source keeps its film moving). Coplanar faces are merged only inside a brick (a calm flat brick is one quad instead of N^2, TestFineFlatBrickIsOneQuad; added after the tables above, not measured); merging across bricks would need T-junction handling on non-flat edges.

N=2 costs about half of N=4 per brick, N=8 about four times as much (TestFineCostReport logs all three). Memory: about 150 bytes per brick plus N^3 bytes of fill (2048 bricks at N=4: about 0.4 MiB). A 65536-write burst (scattered sources) costs at most 10.3 ms per step (TestSlowFineWorstBurst): past 8192 writes in one step the wake-ups become chunk scans.

Surface mesh (go test ./mesh -bench FineWater): a chunk with 256 bricks of random fills (the worst case: every column has skirts) builds in 4.9 ms, 26940 vertices (105 per brick); a calm, flat brick costs 4 vertices per sub-column.

Coarse mode, for reference (go test -tags slow ./fluid -run 'TestSlowCoarse' -v): filling a 201x10x401 sea (804k writes) costs 0.9 ms/tick on average over 501 ticks, worst 13 ms; breaching its 401x10 wall 0.41 ms/tick, worst 9 ms. The coarse mode has no evaluation cap (an earlier MaxEval made the future depend on stale wake-ups); what bounds a burst is the overflow of the wake-up queue to chunk scans, now at 8192 writes in both modes (it was 65536): a burst of 65536 scattered sources went from one 257 ms step (983k cells evaluated) to a worst step of 30 ms (83k cells). No golden or recorded world reached either threshold, so their hashes are unchanged.

Limits#

  • Pressure acts through full cells only and moves at most one layer per body per step; a body larger than FinePressure cells only equalises locally. Sources do not take part in pressure.
  • The coarse/fine seam has a tolerance of one level (1/8 block): fine water can rest up to that much above or below a calm coarse neighbour.
  • Folding may move less than one level of water inside a calm connected body (never across a wall, never changing the total).
  • A spill refused by a cap keeps its brick dirty, so a world at FineMaxBricks is not idle until the overload folds have made room (bounded cost, no growth). Scenes that keep moving (springs filling a closed cave, terraces draining into a closed pool) stay at the cap while they move: the water there spreads at the rate the pool and the folds allow.
  • A forced fold turns the front of a sheet into coarse cells for a while: the look changes from the fine surface to the coarse one there until the front is refined again.
  • Streams: the water in the air is not stored in a .voxworld (a load replaces it, ledgered as displaced); a fall deeper than 256 cells lands in mid-air (a new brick, then a new stream below it, starting from rest); the fluid bounds stop at y=512 by default (Config.Max: a pillar taller than that loses its spring into the void); the water of a stream lands in the column of its lip (no horizontal drift in the simulation: the curtain's bend is drawn, not simulated); a drop of a single cell still spawns the brick below.
  • Rendering: the cascade is drawn after the scene copy, so SSR on the pool does not reflect it; at sunset the aerated curtain takes the sky's lilac ambient and looks a little too bright next to the shadowed cliff.
  • A pool of coarse sources is drawn full but holds the bricks beside it at its constant head (a quarter block at the default level): where the two meet, the source cell slopes down to the bricks over one block.
  • Lateral spreading is diffusive (half the difference per sub-cell pair and step): under a strong spring the water still heaps into a low mound around the impact before it spreads (drawn as one smooth sheet).

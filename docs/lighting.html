Lighting: block light and skylight#

Burrow lights the voxel world with a propagated, coloured light field: block light (red, green and blue channels, 4 bits each) from emitters such as torches, lava and glowstone, and skylight (4 bits) from the open sky. It is derived data: a pure function of the blocks and the registry, never part of StateHash, a snapshot or a journal.

What was there before#

voxel.Block.Emit and the default emitters by name (voxel/light.go) existed, and the mesher flood-filled a 64^3 window around every chunk to give each vertex a coloured light (mesh.Opts.BlockLight). There was no skylight (caves were lit by the hemispheric ambient, so a cave at night looked like the night outside), no attenuation by block, no persistent field (every edit re-meshed the 26 neighbours and recomputed the window), no query API, no soft renderer light and only 3 blue bits. Ambient occlusion existed (per-vertex AO in the mesh, SSAO or ray traced AO in the post stack) and is unchanged.

The rules#

  • A light level is 0..15 per channel. Entering a non-opaque block costs one level per channel plus the block's filter; opaque blocks stop light (an opaque emitter lights its neighbours).
  • Block light: Block.Emit (0..15) and a colour. Known names (torch, soul_torch, redstone_torch, lava, glowstone, sea_lantern, lantern, the froglights, ...) have a default level and colour (voxel.Block.Emission, state suffixes such as torch[wall=true] are ignored). Block.EmitColor overrides the colour. The colour is mixed 30% toward white so every channel holds levels for most of the range (a saturated colour would burn its weak channels close to the source and leave only a thin tail). A warm light still turns redder with distance because every channel loses the same number of levels.
  • Skylight: 15 at the top of the topmost chunk layer, straight down it loses nothing, sideways one level per block. A roof therefore makes a dark room whose edges are lit.
  • Filters (Block.LightLoss = extra levels per channel; default by name, Block.LightFilter): leaves 1/1/1, water 2/1/1 (red goes first), non-opaque ice 1/1/0, stained glass keeps its own colour and absorbs the others (a red pane passes red). Shaped blocks do not block light (a slab does not shade).
  • Chunk borders are transparent to the algorithm; cells next to unloaded chunks are lit as if the unloaded space were solid until it loads.

voxel.LightField#

Per chunk, 16 bits per cell (r | g<<4 | b<<8 | sky<<12), uniform chunks (open sky) store one value. Updates are incremental breadth-first searches per channel:

  • Add: an emitter or a freed cell queues its neighbours; a cell brighter than a neighbour minus the cost raises it.
  • Remove: removing a block or an emitter queues the old value; neighbours that could only have been lit through it are cleared and the brighter neighbours around the hole are queued again to refill it (the classic two queue algorithm, also for skylight columns).
  • Edits are detected by diffing a chunk against the classes it was lit with (Chunk.Version), so undo, redo, restore, fluid and script edits all work without a hook.
  • Chunk load / unload: a loaded chunk pulls from its loaded neighbours and pushes into them; an unloaded chunk removes the light that flowed out of it.

The result is the fixpoint of max-propagation: independent of load order, edit order and budget slices (tests compare random edit sequences with a from scratch flood fill, cell by cell). Step(budget) does at most budget cell visits and keeps its queues; removals always run before additions. A torch placed and removed in a 85x27x85 cave costs about 0.6 ms (BenchmarkLightCaveUpdate) and TestLightUpdateBudgetBigCave bounds the visits.

Where it runs (frame domain, off the main thread)#

The streamer (engine/lightrun.go) owns one LightField on its own goroutine, fed by columns loading and unloading and by chunk touches (markDirty, plus a sweep of a few columns per frame for edits that bypass the controller). It reads blocks through the world read lock (ChunkMap.View). The main thread only sends commands and applies results, so propagation costs the main thread nothing (frame time p99 with light on equals light off, see Measurements).

Mesh workers copy a padded light snapshot of their chunk (LightField.Snapshot, under a read lock held for at most one 8192 visit slice) and the mesher reads it (mesh.Opts.Light, per-vertex smooth light = the mean of the four open cells around each corner, exactly as before, now with the sky channel). Meshes are not delayed: a chunk meshed while the field is still settling uses a provisional estimate computed by the mesher (mesh/lightest.go, Opts.EstimateLight): the field's own rules run on the padded chunk (skylight falls down every column, from the sky or through the layers above the chunk read from the world, attenuated by leaves and water, nothing under a solid block, then spreads sideways; emitters inside the window flood). Each mesh records a hash of the light it read (Result.LightHash, solid cells zeroed, like LightField.Snapshot); when the field settles the runner re-meshes only the chunks whose hash differs (sources beyond the one-cell border), so a good estimate costs nothing and a change on a chunk border reaches only the neighbours that read it. Reports are flushed when the field is idle or every 4 s of continuous work; loads are sent nearest first.

Simulation queries#

ChunkMap.LightAt(reg, p) (also on voxel.Access, sim-side code, nav and scripts) is the deterministic query: a separate sim-side field that lights the 3x3 columns around the queried cell synchronously, re-reads edited chunks and returns the exact value of the infinite world (light cannot travel 32 blocks, so a neighbourhood is enough). It depends on the blocks only: not on streaming radius, camera, frame rate or call order. It is never hashed.

Light{R,G,B,Sky}  .Block() = max(R,G,B)   .Level(skyDim) = max(Block, Sky - skyDim)   skyDim 0 at noon .. 15 at night
  • Scripts: host import light_at(x,y,z) -> packed r|g<<4|b<<8|sky<<12 (ABI.md), Go SDK burrowsdk.LightAt(x,y,z) (Light, error). It sees the committed blocks, not the writes buffered by the running call.
  • Nav / AI: behaviour tree condition dark{max=7, sky_dim} (nav.Builtins) and nav.View.LightAt; ai.Dark(light, skyDim, max) is the pure rule. "Mobs spawn in darkness": dark on the cell a spawner picks, with sky_dim taken from the (deterministic) day clock.
  • MCP: vox_light {pos, sky_dim} (burrowd) and editor_light {pos, sky_dim, view}.

Layers: skylight enters through the top of the highest generated chunk layer (engine sets it from the generator, default 0..1). Chunks outside the layer range (edits far above the terrain) are not lit; blocks there neither shade nor emit.

Rendering#

The vertex stays 16 bytes. Position uses bits 0..9 of x, y, z (0..512 in sixteenths), so the high bits of x carry the skylight and those of y the blue light (red and green stay above the AO bits of the vertex word). Dynamic voxel bodies keep raw positions (pc.b.x = 2). An unlit vertex (mesh.Vertex.Lit false) reads as open sky.

Shaders (voxel_body.glsl, water.glsl, water_hdr.frag, all shader levels including off, and -look classic):

  • ambient is multiplied by skyOcc(sky) (1 in the open, 0.012 + 0.988 * sky^1.7 below), direct sun by skyVis(sky): caves are dark by day and at night (the existing sky/sun/moon cycle already scales the ambient, so "skylight scaled by time of day" is the ambient times the skylight);
  • block light adds blockRadiance = 2.4 * light^2.2 (HDR levels, per channel) or 0.95 * light^1.7 (display space looks) times the albedo and AO;
  • the environment reflection is scaled by the sky occlusion.

Smooth lighting and AO: the corner light is the mean of the open cells (an occluded corner averages fewer, brighter-or-darker cells), the AO factor multiplies ambient and block light in the shader. Quads merge only when their four corner lights are equal.

The soft renderer has the same terms (render/soft/light.go, render.Options.Light; the engine passes an on-demand voxel.DemandLight), so headless renders and the parity tests match: make view-test parity 5.3 / 4.9 / 8.0, shadows off 4.1, classic 6.5 (thresholds 7.0 / 6.6 / 8.8 / 5.4 / 8.0).

Ray tracing. Emitters already bounce light through the GI ray (RTBlockInfo.Emit, now with the emission colour instead of the block colour). The traced passes scale the ambient share of the lit pixel, and the baked block light is added by the raster shader, so GI keeps working; the tracer does not trace the block light field itself (documented limit: no traced shadows from torches, no GI from block light onto other surfaces beyond the emitter bounce).

Environment components: time, sun, sky, weather, fog, post#

The look of a scene's sky and air is made of ordinary components on ordinary nodes. New projects and templates put one Environment node in their first scene (builtin:environment.prefab, scene.DefaultEnvironmentPrefab: env.time starting at 0.35, env.sun, env.sky). Fog and post can also sit on zone nodes.

What is hashed. Only env.time and env.weather are simulation state (they drive the clock and the weather, so they are in the StateHash and the journal like any component). env.sun, env.sky, env.fog and env.post are renderer-only: never hashed, never journaled, never read by a FixedUpdate system. Adding them to a scene changes no hash. The blended result lives in the frame-domain engine.EnvState resource.

Volumes and blending. env.fog and env.post carry a volume: shape global (everywhere), box (half extents size in the node's space, follows its transform) or sphere (radius size.x). Inside the volume the weight is 1 (times weight), and it fades to 0 over blend blocks outside, measured from the active camera. Volumes blend in priority order, higher last so it wins, ties by node order: fog density and the post offsets interpolate toward each volume by its weight (scene.BlendFog, scene.BlendPost; a zero fog colour keeps the colour below). env.time, env.sun, env.sky and env.weather are global: the first node in tree order that carries one wins. scene.ResolveEnv(nodes, cam, tick) does all of it; internal/game calls it every frame for the game camera and the editor for the editor camera.

Scene frames drawn through rworld (the hybrid mesh pass, character and model previews) take the same fields from rworld.ApplyEnv: a clock without a DirectionalLight node drives the sun, a DirectionalLight keeps its direction and colour but takes the gain, tint and shadow switch, env.sky and env.fog replace the sky and aerial perspective colours.

The editor resolves the same components for the editor camera each frame (editor/core/env.go, pushed to the renderer by editor/gpu through engine.EnvApplier): the viewport shows the time of day, the sun, the sky, the fog volumes around the camera and the post grade of the open scene exactly like the game, and a change shows on the next frame (the scene is re-read every few frames). Nothing of it is an undo step.

Old scene.settings fields#

time_of_day, exposure, fill_light and music no longer exist in scene.settings (a scene that still has them is rejected). burrow migrate turns them into an Environment node (env.time frozen at that time, env.sun, env.sky, env.post) and a Music node (audio.music); the scene looks and sounds the same.

Scene settings: controller and hud#

A node of the scene (a root or a direct child of a root) can carry the scene.settings component; the running scene applies it (start scene, and again after a scene change, checked a few times a second by internal/game/scene_settings.go). All fields are optional, absent means "project default":

"scene.settings": {"controller": false, "hud": false}
fieldeffect
controllerfalse suspends the first-person controller and the player avatar for this scene only (no movement, hotbar, break/place; the app paces the ticks itself). true or absent leaves the project setting. The scene also frees the mouse cursor (menu mode, clicks go to the UI) while it runs and takes it back when the next scene turns the controller on; a game UI modal keeps managing the pointer itself. With runtime.plugins.controller: false, any scene that says true makes the project install the controller plugin anyway (found by scanning the scene and prefab files for scene.settings with "controller": true); it stays suspended in every scene that does not say true. A project with no such scene is unchanged (nothing is installed, StateHash identical).
hudfalse hides crosshair, hotbar and text overlay, true forces them.

Settings precedence: command-line flag, then BURROW_* environment variable, then vox.project, then the engine default. scene.settings is not a fourth source of the same values: it only switches the controller and the HUD while its scene runs (the project value applies again in the next scene). Everything except controller is frame domain (the engine.SceneRuntime resource): never hashed, never journaled. The component is registered in the default schema and not hashed, so adding it to a scene does not change the StateHash.

Scene-stored voxel edits (world.edits)#

"world.edits": {"boxes": [{"min": [10,64,10], "max": [14,70,14], "block": ""}, {"min": [12,64,12], "max": [12,64,12], "block": "campfire"}]}

Boxes are applied in order, once, when the scene is first seen as the running one, as ordinary journaled fill_box commands (so they are part of the StateHash, replay and undo like any edit). block empty or air clears. Corners may be in either order. Limits: 64 boxes, 1,048,576 cells per box, 4,194,304 in total, coordinates within +-1,048,576; violations are rejected when the scene is decoded. An unknown block name skips that box with a warning.

Debug view, switches#

  • BURROW_LIGHT=off or RenderSettings.NoLight turn the light off (meshes carry no light, shaders read open sky).
  • Light debug view (frame domain): RenderSettings.LightView / RenderPlugin.SetLightView / editor View menu "Light view" / editor_light {view}: block (colour of the block light), sky (skylight), level (brightness heat map). The soft renderer has it too (render.Options.LightView).
  • Demos: examples/voxel_sandbox -demo lightcave|lightcolors|lavalake -shapes -tick 18000 (night; -tick 6000 for day).

Measurements (M2 Max; the machine was shared, load average about 11, so absolute numbers are indicative)#

  • Mesh memory: the GPU vertex stays 16 bytes. Default heightmap terrain radius 3: 176 280 -> 190 356 opaque vertices (+8.0%) (TestLightMeshGrowth, limit 25%).
  • 1440p, radius 8, -gen v2, medium: GPU 5.82 ms with and without light, 1.064 M vs 1.045 M triangles in view (+1.8%).
  • Frame time while flying and sprinting (-novsync, 1280x720, p99 of 1380 frames): 9.7 and 12.3 ms with light, 13.0 and 13.3 ms without; walking 11.7 / 12.2 vs 11.3 / 11.8. Uploads while flying 73.8 MiB with light vs 37.0 MiB without (2.0x; 3.9x with the open-sky provisional light, 5-6x before the hash filter). Initial load at radius 16 (2 layers): settled after 2.35 s vs 1.24 s without light (was 11.9 s); TestLightStreamingUploadsStayLow keeps the upload ratio under 1.8x (1.4x measured).
  • Light field cost (BenchmarkLightCaveUpdate): place plus remove a torch in a big cave 0.58 ms; the initial light of a chunk is a column fill plus seeds from the cells that can still brighten a neighbour (uniform open-sky chunks cost nothing in memory).

Limits#

  • Shaped blocks do not partially block light; skylight does not model partial occlusion by slabs and stairs.
  • Light is not shadowed by entities, and scene meshes / dynamic voxel bodies do not emit or receive propagated light (bodies read open sky).
  • A class table holds 255 distinct (opaque, filter, emission) behaviours; beyond that further ones read as air. Registry.Update of a light property needs LightField.Reset.
  • Cells at the edge of the streamed area are lit as if the unloaded space were solid (block light) or open (the provisional estimate in meshes).

Voxel size#

Light is measured in metres, not voxels. The field keeps each channel in units of 1/Scale level (LightConfig.Scale = the world's voxels per metre: 1, 2, 4 or 8): an emitter holds level * Scale units, every cell step costs one unit (plus the block's filter), and sky enters at 15 * Scale. Queries, snapshots, light_at and the renderer still read the levels 0..15 (units floored to whole levels), so a torch lights the same radius in metres at every voxel size. At scale 1 a unit is a level and the field is byte-identical to before. The cost is memory: a materialised chunk of light holds four 8-bit channels (128 KiB per 32^3 chunk, was 64 KiB), and the on-demand sampler loads ceil(15 * Scale / 32) chunks around the cell (1 up to scale 2, 2 at scale 4, 4 at scale 8).

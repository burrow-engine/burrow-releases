Weather#

burrow-engine/weather is a deterministic weather simulation: a seeded state machine of fronts (clear, cloudy, fog, light rain, heavy rain, storm, snow), smoothed intensities, a slowly turning wind, a day clock with an optional seasonal cycle, lightning and thunder, and a per-position sampler (At) that turns the front into the weather felt at one place. This page is the simulation foundation and its API; rendering the weather (visible rain and snow, lightning flashes, fog, wet surfaces, wind on clouds and leaves, overcast sky) is the shader stage S3 (rendering.md), which reads the weather.State described below through weather.RenderFX. The simulation never depends on the renderer.

Enabling it, and why it is off by default#

weather.Plugin{Options{Enabled: true, Seasons: true}} installs the module; the zero Options installs it inert: nothing evolves, At answers "clear", and the StateHash of the app is bit for bit what it was without the plugin (an inert extra contributes nothing to the hash). That keeps every existing project, golden and journal valid. It is on where a game wants it:

wherehow
burrowplay / game projects"runtime": {"weather": true, "seasons": true} in vox.project (the sandbox template sets both)
codeapp.AddPlugin(&weather.Plugin{Options: weather.Options{Enabled: true}}) after ScenePlugin
editor, burrowd sessionsalways installed, disabled; turn it on with editor_weather {enabled:true}, the View > Weather panel, or vox_act weather_set
a bare sim.Engineweather.Install(e, seed, opts) from sim.Config.Setup

A journal recorded with weather enabled replays only on an engine that installs the module (the editor, burrowd and the app plugin all do).

Model#

All evolving state is integer (Q16 fixed point, permille, splitmix64 of (world seed, counter, salt)); there is no wall clock, no math/rand, no floating point in the state, so every platform reaches the same hash. Floats exist only in the frame-domain State / Local values.

  • Fronts. A front (Kind) lasts a drawn number of ticks (20 ticks = 1 s): clear 5 to 20 min, cloudy 4 to 14 min, fog 2 to 6 min, light rain 3 to 10 min, heavy rain 2 to 7 min, storm 1.5 to 5 min, snow 3 to 10 min. The next front is drawn from a transition table (clear -> cloudy -> rain -> clearing; storms end in heavy rain; fog burns off), adjusted by the mean temperature (no snow above 2 C, snow x4 below 0 C, no storm below 4 C) and the hour (morning fog x3). Each front draws its targets: precipitation, cloud cover, fog, humidity.
  • Smoothing. The current values move toward the targets by a bounded step per tick (precipitation 0 to 1 over 60 s, cover 45 s, fog 75 s), so a change of front never jumps. Surface wetness rises under rain and dries over about 9 minutes.
  • Wind. Every 30 s a new target heading (at most 45 degrees from the previous) and speed (3 to 17 m/s plus a boost from the front) is drawn; the heading slews at 2.7 deg/s, the speed slowly. Heading is where the wind blows toward, radians in the XZ plane (0 = +X, pi/2 = +Z).
  • Lightning and thunder. In a storm with precipitation >= 0.5 a strike occurs with probability proportional to the precipitation (about one every 6 s at full intensity), at a drawn bearing and distance (80 to 2480 blocks) from the listener. Thunder arrives distance / 343 m/s later (up to 4 pending). Both are state (StrikeSeq, ThunderSeq), and the plugin turns new ones into LightningEvent / ThunderEvent.
  • Time and seasons. Clock is the day clock in ticks, starting at 6000 (sunrise, the renderer's existing sky offset): scene.NewSky(State.Clock) is the matching sky. A day is 24000 ticks (20 min). With Seasons the temperature follows a 12 day year (288000 ticks) of +-9 C.
  • Temperature. 10 C mean + seasonal + diurnal (+-4 C, warmest at 14:24) + climate noise (worldgen.ClimateAt, +-8 C) - 0.15 C per block above RefY (20) - cover and precipitation cooling (up to 3 C). Integer milli-degrees; rain falls as snow where Temp <= SnowC (0 C), so one front can be snow on a mountain and rain in the valley. There is no biome system yet, so the climate noise stands for biomes; a sand-storm front is not modelled (no desert biome exists).

API#

st := weather.Res(ctx)                  // *weather.State, refreshed each frame (Update stage); also valid from Startup
l  := st.At(pos)                        // weather.Local at a world position (no allocation)
ex := weather.ExposureAt(st, blocks, reg, blockPos) // sheltered? under rain/snow? wet?
st.SkyLightFactor(); st.SkyLightLevel(15) // cloud/precipitation dimming for a sky-light level

weather.State (frame domain, plain copyable values): Enabled, Frozen, TimeFrozen, Kind, Intensity (precipitation 0..1), Cover, Fog, Humidity, Wetness, Wind{Dir, Speed, Vec, Gust}, Temp, Clock, DayTime (0..1), Day, Season, TicksLeft, Strike and Thunder ({Seq, Tick, Bearing, Dist}), Overcast, and FlashAt(tick) (lightning illumination envelope 0..1).

weather.Local (from At): Kind (snow only where it is cold enough), Phase (none/rain/snow), Intensity, Temp, Humidity, Cover, Fog, Wetness, Wind, Lightning (a storm in the rain phase), RainSupply, SnowSupply, Extinguish.

ExposureAt scans the voxel column above the point (up to MaxSkyScan = 96 blocks) for a Solid or Opaque block: Sheltered, CeilingY, UnderRain, UnderSnow, Wet (open sky under rain >= 0.15), Wetness. It is deterministic and does not allocate.

Gameplay hooks (the sim does not edit voxels)#

The repository has no fire and no sky-light level yet, so those are hooks, not behaviour: Local.Extinguish (rain strong enough to put a fire out) and State.SkyLightFactor() (0.3..1) are what a fire system or a sky-light consumer should read, from FixedUpdate through weather.Of(sim).At(pos) (journal-safe: it reads state only). Local.RainSupply / SnowSupply (0..1) are the rain inflow hook for the water simulation (W1): the water layer reads them per column; weather itself never places water, snow cover or ice. Snow accumulation and ice on water are out of scope here and are extensions tied to the water/physics layer.

Commands, editor and agents#

One journaled command, weather_set (weather.Set), with optional fields: enabled, type (clear|cloudy|fog|light_rain|heavy_rain|storm|snow), intensity 0..1 (precipitation for rain/storm/snow, fog density for fog, cloud cover for clear/cloudy), cover, wind_dir (degrees), wind_speed (m/s, holds the wind until wind_auto), time (day fraction), freeze (stop the front), freeze_time (stop the clock), seasons, instant (skip the smoothing), plan (the weather plan, below) and day_length (seconds per day, 60 and up). Anything but enabled needs the weather enabled. Validation happens before any change; a failed command leaves no trace. weather_restore replaces the whole state (the inverse used by undo).

  • Editor: Tools > Environment is a full-screen tool: the world with a wind compass (drag the dial to turn the wind), the Day strip (a 24 h band coloured by the sky, a sun marker you drag to set the time of day, and the plan lane), and Environment settings on the right (weather cards, intensity, wind, plan segments, time, start time, day length, freeze). The header switch (Frozen, Live x1, x60, x600) previews the time of day and the plan running in the editor at that speed: it is not saved, makes no undo step and no dirty flag, and leaving the tool, editing, or pressing Play puts the edited time and weather back (the preview is not journaled; strike flashes do not animate in it). Freeze time stays a separate setting that Play and the game obey. One change or one slider drag is one undo step; it is journaled, replayed by editor_verify_replay and restored exactly by Stop. The weather is a runtime setting of the world: saving a scene does not store it, except the plan and the day length, which go to environment.json.

The weather plan#

Params.Plan is a schedule of segments over the day: {"from":250,"to":380,"type":"light_rain","intensity":600} (from and to are permille of the day, 0..1000, sorted and not overlapping; type is a kind name; intensity is permille, as the intensity of weather_set). While the day clock is inside a segment the front is that segment's kind (the random fronts are held, Sim.PlanSeg says which segment runs); between segments the weather changes by itself as before. Entering a segment starts its front with the smoothing of any other change. At most 24 segments. Set it with weather_set {"plan":[...]} (an empty list clears it). The editor writes the plan and day_length to environment.json at the root of the project and reads them back when the project opens; a game that installs weather.Plugin sets Options.Params.Plan itself.

environment.json and the game#

environment.json is {"enabled":true,"plan":[...],"day_length":1200,"start_time":0.25} (weather.Env). start_time is the time of day the game and the editor's Simulate (Play) start at, a day fraction (0 midnight, 0.25 sunrise, 0.5 noon); it is Params.ClockStart, set with weather_set {"start_time":0.5} (changing only day_length keeps the start fraction). A standalone game (burrowplay, the editor's Play window) that finds the file loads it into the weather.Plugin options (Env.Apply): the weather is on unless enabled is false (older files without the field mean on), the plan runs, and the sky, time and rain use the same render.WeatherFX as the editor. Without the file the game behaves as before (runtime.weather).

  • MCP: editor_weather (same fields; no arguments reads; at:[x,y,z] also samples the local weather). burrowd/burrowctl: vox_act with {"kind":"weather_set","data":{...}}.

Determinism contract#

The plan (Params.Plan, Sim.PlanSeg) is part of the hashed state, so enabling the weather gives a different StateHash than before the plan existed (the 100k-tick golden changed once).

StateHash includes the weather (type, counters, targets, smoothed values, wind, strikes, clock, parameters, seed) when it is enabled, in the engine extra "weather"; it is rolled back with a failed tick, snapshotted and restored, and replayed from the journal. Tests: 100k tick golden hash, no per-tick jump, transition statistics, snow only below the threshold, journal replay, snapshot/restore, undo, zero allocation per tick (weather/*_test.go, editor/core/weather_test.go). A snapshot taken before the module was installed does not restore into an engine that has it (the extra count differs); this only affects snapshots saved by an older build.

Time of day without the weather simulation#

An env.time node with frozen: true (a day fraction, 0 midnight, 0.25 sunrise, 0.5 noon, 0.75 sunset) freezes the sky and sun of the running scene without runtime.weather: the renderer builds scene.NewSky(time * 24000) from the frame-domain engine.SceneRuntime, the weather module stays inert, the clock does not advance and the StateHash is bit for bit the same as without the setting (internal/game test TestSceneSettingsApplyAndKeepStateHash). It overrides the weather clock while the scene runs. See lighting.md.

Environment components and the weather#

env.time and env.weather (lighting.md) are the declarative way to set the clock and the front. Both are hashed (simulation state): the running game resolves them each frame and hands the clock and the front to this module as journaled weather_set commands (weather.SetFromEnv), once per change, so they replay and enter the StateHash like any weather command. env.time sets the day clock (start, day_length, frozen), env.weather holds a front (kind, intensity, wind, frozen) and works with the weather module enabled or not. env.sky.clouds, env.fog and env.sun are renderer-only and never reach the module: a thick env.fog volume thickens the aerial perspective on top of the weather fog (WeatherFX.Fog takes the larger), env.sky.clouds on/off replaces the cloud cover the weather asks for. The editor viewport shows env.time as the sky time; it does not run the weather from it.

What the shader stage (S3) consumes#

The plugin's weather.render system (PostUpdate) reads weather.Res(ctx) once per frame, samples st.At(camera position) and writes the frame resource *render.WeatherFX (weather.RenderFX(st, cam, tick)); RenderPlugin hands it to the Vulkan renderer, and the editor viewport computes it for the editor camera. All levels low..ultra draw it; -shaders off ignores it.

effectinputs
sky timeState.Clock -> scene.NewSky(Clock) when Enabled (otherwise keep the existing sky tick)
cloud cover / overcastState.Cover (target for -clouds auto; the CLI -clouds keeps priority), State.Overcast, Local.Cover
rain / snow particlesLocal.Phase, Local.Intensity, Wind.Vec (slant), temperature for flake vs drop; draw only where the column is open (ExposureAt or a height map)
fog / mistLocal.Fog, Local.Humidity, State.Kind == KindFog
wet surfacesState.Wetness / Local.Wetness (darker albedo, higher specular on opaque, non-sheltered pixels)
lightningState.FlashAt(tick) (global fill light + sky), State.Strike{Seq,Bearing,Dist} for the bolt, LightningEvent / ThunderEvent for audio
wind on clouds and foliageWind.Vec, Wind.Speed, Wind.Gust (0..1), Wind.Dir
precipitation sizerender.WeatherFX.Unit (voxels per metre, weather.RenderFXUnit): drop length and width, fall speeds, wind drift and the spawn box are in metres; the precipitation height map spans 64 m at every voxel size (engine.PrecipGrid: 64 x 64 texels of 1 voxel at 1 voxel per metre, up to 256 x 256 texels of several voxels, a texel being roofed by any of its columns)
ambient dimmingSkyLightFactor() on the sun term (the CPU twin scene.Sky.Atmo(cover) already takes a cover)

Limits and extensions#

  • No biome system: the climate is worldgen.ClimateAt (render-tint noise) plus altitude. A biome table would replace Sim.at's two inputs.
  • No sand storm (no desert biome), no snow cover, no ice on water, no fire, no per-entity wetness component; the hooks above are in place.
  • Lightning is global (bearing and distance from the listener), not tied to a world column; a bolt that ignites or electrifies a block is a gameplay layer on top of StrikeSeq.
  • Rendering (S3): snow does not accumulate, the bolt is a sky effect (no ground strike), BURROW_LIGHTNING=<deg> forces a bolt for captures.

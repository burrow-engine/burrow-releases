Rendering#

Rendering is frame-domain: it reads a snapshot of the simulation and never changes it.

flowchart LR
  S["Simulation snapshot"] --> X["RenderWorldPlugin<br/>extracts render.* components"]
  S --> T["Terrain<br/>greedy-meshed chunks"]
  X --> H["Hybrid frame<br/>render/vulkan + render/rworld"]
  T --> H
  H --> U["Canvas<br/>HUD and UI"]
  U --> W["Window"]
  S --> P["render/soft<br/>headless screenshots"]
  • Vulkan renderer (render/vulkan, Vulkan 1.3 dynamic rendering, on macOS through MoltenVK): voxel terrain with greedy-meshed chunks, ambient occlusion, a shadow cascade, water, sky and fog, HUD; plus the scene frame (render/rworld): cameras, directional and point lights, mesh instances, sprites (optionally billboards), voxel models, PBR-lite materials (Lambert + emissive, alpha opaque/blend/mask) and custom SPIR-V shaders. Terrain and scene render in one hybrid frame, so meshes cast and receive terrain shadows.
  • RenderPlugin drives the window; RenderWorldPlugin extracts the scene components every frame (render.camera, render.dirlight, render.pointlight, render.mesh, render.sprite, render.voxmodel, render.material). With no active camera node the player's camera is used.
  • Canvas (render/canvas) is the 2D layer used by the HUD and the UI toolkit (ui): rects, lines, images, text. It has a Vulkan backend and a deterministic CPU rasterizer (render/canvas/soft) used for golden-image tests.
  • CPU renderers for headless work: render/soft (voxel raycaster, used by SoftRenderPlugin, burrowshot and the editor/agent screenshots without a GPU).

Distance level of detail (chunk LOD)#

Far chunk columns are meshed coarser, so the view distance stays usable and a world with smaller voxels does not pay 64x the geometry per volume. A column is drawn at LOD 0 (full detail, the ordinary mesher), LOD 1 (2x2x2 voxels per cell), LOD 2 (4^3) or LOD 3 (8^3), chosen from the horizontal distance to the camera in metres (mesh.LODConfig): defaults 160 / 320 / 640 m, with an 8% hysteresis band so a camera on a ring edge does not re-mesh back and forth. Chunks of a column share the level.

  • A LOD cell is solid when at least half of its voxels are (surface error under one cell), liquid when at least half are liquid; its block is the top-most solid voxel, so the surface keeps its grass or sand colour. LOD 1 faces are textured (the block texture sampled per voxel like the near mesh, mips averaging it; LODOpts.TexLOD, no extra vertices), LOD 2 and 3 keep flat averaged colours (nothing shimmers). LOD meshes use the normal vertex format, no light and no ray tracing data, and go through the same terrain passes and fog. Shadows reach only as far as the shadow cascades do.
  • Cross-fade: a column that changes level is re-meshed and the renderer keeps its old mesh for 350 ms (mesh.Mesh.Fade, a ghost with every part: opaque, cut-out, glass and water) while both are drawn through a screen-door dither with complementary thresholds (pc.b.z, lodfade.glsl): no pop, no blending pass, no extra vertices. Glass and water use the same dither (each pixel is kept or discarded, never blended twice), so the fade needs no extra sorting. Shadows: both meshes cast while they dissolve, through the same dither in the shadow map (shadowcut.frag; a fading chunk leaves the depth-only fast path for the alpha-test pipeline) and the shadow filter blends the two silhouettes; switching the caster at the midpoint would pop the whole shadow in one frame. Edits and first loads do not fade.
  • Seams: the outer ring of cells keeps a cell solid as soon as one voxel is, and every solid border cell emits a full-height wall, so chunks of different levels close each other without cracks (TestLODSeamsWatertight).
  • Meshing runs on the stream workers from the full-detail data; the base cache that holds it is bounded (baseCacheChunks, 4096 columns at most however far the view reaches).
  • API: VoxelWorld.SetLOD(mesh.LODConfig{VoxelSize: 0.25, ...}) or SetVoxelSize(0.25) (metres per voxel; rings keep their size in the world), LODConfig.Off for full detail everywhere. Debug: BURROW_LOD=0 turns the default off, BURROW_LOD_RINGS="160,320,640" sets the rings.
  • Coarse sampling. A generator that is a height field (worldgen.Heightmap, worldgen.Flat, worldgen.Biomes, and worldgen.Scaled over a sampled generator) implements worldgen.ColumnSource: the streamer then meshes the LOD 2 and LOD 3 chunks from column samples (worldgen.CoarseField, one lookup per cell, the same half-solid and border rules) and does not generate the full-detail chunks of those columns or of their neighbours (about 5-7x less work per chunk, and the base cache stays small). It falls back to the full generation when the generator cannot be sampled, when an edited chunk lies within one chunk of the build, and after SetWorldGenHints swaps the generator. Caves lie below the dirt layers and are not seen at those levels. Trees and plants are left out of the samples (a tree is a stray cell or two at 4-8 voxels per cell): they appear at LOD 0 and 1 only. Biomes samples its terrain model per column (ground, water level, surface rule from the four-neighbour slope; no ice, ores or vegetation); Scaled scales the inner column rows by N. Chunks at LOD 0 and 1 are unchanged and Generate (the bytes of every scale) is untouched. MaxRadius is 96 columns: the ray tracing table (256 x 32 x 256, docs/ray-tracing.md) holds 2(r+2)+1 columns without aliasing up to 125, LOD chunks carry no ray tracing data, and the base cache is capped at 4096 columns whatever the radius.

Block textures (terrain)#

The terrain draws the embedded block textures (assets/blocktex, see texture-packs.md) with LabPBR shading. Flags: -textures default|none, -pbr=false (albedo only), -texfilter nearest|linear, F5 toggles textures in the sandbox, -gallery shows every textured block in a grid (examples/voxel_sandbox).

Texture scale and distances in metres (small voxels)#

A world with 1/2, 1/4 or 1/8 m voxels (runtime.generator.voxel_size) draws every block texture per voxel by default (texture_scale: "voxel"), so a stone tile is 4x smaller at 0.25 m. texture_scale: "metre" (world dialog of the editor, runtime.generator.texture_scale, editor/core.GenParams) samples the textures in world space instead: one tile spans one metre (n voxels). The mesher (mesh.Opts.MetreTex) keeps the UVs in voxel units and adds the voxel position along the texture axes modulo n (mesh/tilescale.go), so faces of one block are seamless across voxels, merged quads and chunk borders; the vertex shader divides by pc.b.y (Mesh.TexDiv, per chunk) and the REPEAT sampler of the texture array wraps. Slabs, stairs and other solid shape faces tile the same way; plants and other planes keep one texture per voxel. At most 16 - n blocks are merged per fine quad (UV byte). It is render only (not hashed) and does nothing at 1 m voxels.

Distances are in metres whatever the voxel size: RenderPlugin.Far, a scene camera's far and env.fog distance (fog end; start at a fifth of it) are multiplied by the voxels per metre (engine.MetresToUnits, rworld.Input.Unit); the default far plane follows the streamed radius. Scale 1 is unchanged.

  • Mesh (mesh.Opts.Layers, a mesh.LayerProvider): vertices carry Layer (layer+1, 0 = flat colour), UV in block units (a 4x2 merged quad spans U 0..4, V 0..2; the REPEAT sampler tiles it) and Tinted (Color is then the biome tint). Faces merge only when block, AO and tint agree. U runs along the texture's right axis, V down, seen from outside: side faces up = +Y, top right = +X / up = -Z. Without a provider the mesh is the old flat one.
  • Vulkan (render/vulkan/blocktex.go): three 2D_ARRAY images (one layer per blocktex.Table layer, CPU mip chain uploaded through staging, nothing generated on the GPU): albedo R8G8B8A8_SRGB (the swapchain is sRGB and water blends in linear light, so the hardware decodes before filtering and the shader re-encodes with the exact sRGB curve), normal and specular R8G8B8A8_UNORM. One REPEAT sampler; -texfilter nearest samples the nearest texel and mip, -texfilter linear (default) uses trilinear minification with up to 8x anisotropy (when the device has samplerAnisotropy) and LINEAR magnification that texelAA() in voxel_body.glsl turns back into crisp texels with edges antialiased over one screen pixel (no shimmer on low-resolution textures up close). Voxel vertex is 16 bytes (layer | u<<16 | v<<24). Descriptor set 2 of a dedicated voxel pipeline layout holds the arrays (a neutral 1x1 set is bound when textures are off); push constants are unchanged (pc.a.w = mode 0 flat, 1 albedo, 2 PBR). Only the albedo/normal/spec samplers plus the shadow sampler are used in the voxel fragment shader (4 total).
  • Shader (shaders/voxel.frag): per-face TBN from the face id (no tangent attribute), LabPBR decode (n.xy = rg*2-1, z = sqrt(1-|xy|^2), AO from B at 60 % strength, roughness (1-s)^2, F0 = g/255 for g <= 229, metal for 230..255 with albedo as F0, emission a/254 x albedo, 255 = none), sun Lambert + GGX (Schlick-GGX visibility, Schlick Fresnel) + a sky-gradient reflection scaled by smoothness squared, existing AO, shadow and fog. Cut-out texels (leaves, alpha < 0.5) are discarded. Blocks without a texture (water, unmapped) take the old flat path (layer -1). Glass pass. Solid non-opaque blocks named glass, ice or *_glass (blocktex.Translucent) are drawn in an alpha-blended pass after the water: same shader as the opaque path (shaders/glass.frag = voxel_body.glsl with GLASS), alpha = albedo texel alpha (glass pane 46/255 with an opaque frame and glints, ice 178/255; flat colours 0.35), no depth write, cull off, chunks sorted back to front (per-chunk granularity: two translucent blocks in one chunk can order wrongly). Their frames still cast cut-out shadows. render/soft blends the entry and far face the same way. Limits: water behind glass is drawn before it (right) but glass behind water is blended over it.
  • CPU twin: assets/blocktex/pbr holds DecodeNormal / Roughness / F0 / Emission / Fresnel / GGX / TBN, used by render/soft (set render.Options.Textures; nearest texel, mip from the ray footprint) and unit-tested against hand values. Keep pbr and voxel.frag in sync.
  • Engine: VoxelWorldPlugin.Textures, RenderPlugin.Textures/TexFilter/NoPBR, RenderWorldPlugin.Textures ("" = auto: default pack when compiled in). *engine.BlockTextures (resource) maps registry names to blocktex (BlockTextureName: grass -> grass_block, wood -> oak_log, leaves -> oak_leaves, ...), builds the layer table once and rebuilds (appending layers) when blocks are registered later. SoftRenderPlugin.Textures = "default" opts in.
  • Cost (M2 Max, 16x16, 5 mips): 4 KiB per layer (albedo + normal + spec with mips), 0.16 MiB for the 42 layers of the gallery.

Animated block textures#

A block texture PNG taller than wide (height a whole number of stacked squares) is an animation strip, as Pixel Perfection ships fire, lava, portals or magma, and as a project can ship in textures/block/<name>.png for its runtime.custom_blocks. The frames are kept as extra texture-array layers after the base layers (LayerSet.Anims, blocktex.Anim); the chunk mesh still carries one base layer per face, so animating never re-meshes.

  • Timing: an optional <name>.png.mcmeta next to the PNG (animation.frametime in ticks of 1/20 s, animation.frames for the order and per-frame times). A strip without one plays in order at blocktex.DefaultFrameTime (2 ticks) per frame. Faces with a tinted overlay, glass and ice stay on frame 0.
  • Budget: frames share the 2048-layer array (blocktex.MaxLayers); an animation that does not fit stays on its first frame, at most blocktex.MaxAnims (64) textures animate, and a device whose maxImageArrayLayers is lower than the total uploads the base layers only.
  • GPU: a table of animated base layers (base layer, first frame layer, steps, ticks per step) lives at the end of the scene UBO and animLayer() in shaders/common.glsl picks the layer in the voxel and shadow-cut fragment shaders from S.view.z, the frame's tick.
  • CPU: render/soft uses the same table and blocktex.AnimStep with render.Options.AnimTick; engine screenshots pass the world tick, so a headless shot shows the same frame as the window. AnimTick 0 shows each texture's first frame.
  • The clock is a rendering input only: it is read from the frame state and never written to the simulation or StateHash.
  • Block icons, thumbnails and blocktex.Image always show frame 0.

Screenshots#

burrowplay -shot out.png -frames 60 mygame            # GPU, hidden window, 1280x720 by default
burrowplay -ticks 300 -shot out.png mygame            # warm start: 300 real fixed ticks first, then render
burrowplay -freeze -shot out.png mygame               # reproducible: no sim ticks while the shot settles, sky/water frozen
burrowshot -scenario tower -out tower.png             # CPU renderer, no GPU

-ticks N with a window runs N real fixed ticks first (journaled Step commands, so the run is replayable); -shot always runs at least one so derived state exists in the first frame. Without -freeze the game keeps simulating while the shot settles (-frames frames at one fixed tick each) and the sky/water animate, so the picture depends on -frames. -freeze is explicit: the debug driver feeds zero time (no ticks while settling) and pins the sky and water animation, which is what makes screenshots reproducible. make view-test and the editor selftest use it.

Device limits#

The block texture arrays use one layer per texture: the layer cap is min(2048, VkPhysicalDeviceLimits.maxImageArrayLayers) and a pack over it fails with an error naming both limits (maxImageDimension2D bounds the texture size). none packs are unaffected. Emission mips decode the 255 "none" marker before averaging (glowstone keeps glowing at distance).

Parity and tests#

make render-test renders the showcase scenes on the GPU and compares them with the CPU renderer within measured tolerances; shader and validation-layer errors fail the run (VUID / Validation Error in the log). make view-test does the same for the terrain viewer; both renderers are textured there. Textures raise the Vulkan-vs-soft mean difference (4.6 / 4.8 / 7.2 vs 3.5 / 3.3 / 5.6 flat) because the GPU picks mips per 2x2 quad and soft per ray footprint; the thresholds in the Makefile are measured + 25 %. Textured soft output is deterministic across worker counts (golden keys terrain_tex* in render/soft/testdata/golden.json).

MoltenVK notes#

  • MoltenVK limits shape the renderer (no mutable comparison samplers; FIFO presentation). Always validate changes with the validation layer (make render-test fails on any VUID).
  • burrowplay links MoltenVK directly (libMoltenVK.dylib), so no Vulkan loader is needed to play; the validation layer (-validate) does need the loader and VK_LAYER_PATH.
  • Retina: windows render at the framebuffer size; screenshot runs use point size (NoRetina) for stable output.

See troubleshooting.md for failures.

T3 notes#

  • Tint: albedo = texel * tint * TintGain(1.65) in sRGB space, scaled by 1/max when a channel exceeds 1 (hue-preserving; was a per-channel clip; same in the shader, render/soft and thumbnails; pixel-identical on the default pack views) (see docs/texture-packs.md); tinted-face AO is softened.
  • Shadow map: opaque terrain uses the depth-only pipeline; leaf faces use shadowcut (alpha test on albedo, layer < 0 stays solid). BURROW_CUTSHADOW=0 = old full-block leaf shadows. The soft renderer does not shadow through leaves at all.
  • HUD hotbar uses albedo thumbnails (Options.BlockThumb); target fill is a 0.14 alpha yellow tint on front faces.
  • Debug: BURROW_TEXDEBUG, -keys f5 (sandbox), -pbr=false, -texfilter linear, -gallery all verified.

LOT A: parity, timing, visuals#

  • Soft parity. render.Options.NoShadows (set from -shadows=false) turns soft sun shadows off; leaf/glass shadows in soft are alpha-tested like shadowcut.frag (mip 0 texel alpha < 128 casts no shadow, entry face of a cut-out cluster then its exit face). make view-test mean abs diff: 6.42 / 6.27 / 8.60, shadows off 5.63 (before: 5.51 / 5.94 / 7.56 / 10.53), tolerances measured + 25 % (8.0 / 7.9 / 10.8 / 7.1). The soft Render is worker-count independent (tests).
  • GPU timing. Renderer.Stats().GPU (vulkan.GPUTimes) comes from VK_QUERY_TYPE_TIMESTAMP (8 slots per frame slot, read one to two frames late; needs timestampValidBits > 0 and timestampComputeAndGraphics). Sandbox -frames N prints perf: gpu ms shadow .. main .. total .. | per stage .... On MoltenVK/Apple GPUs stamps inside a render pass resolve at the pass boundary, so sky/opaque/water/hybrid/canvas collapse into the first stage: read shadow and main only. M2 Max, 1280x720, novalidation, camera 0,58,100: radius 8 tex+shadow shadow 0.40 main 1.22 (total 1.62), tex no shadow main 1.25, flat+shadow 0.24 / 0.55, flat no shadow 0.64; radius 12 tex+shadow 0.41 / 1.74 (2.15), tex no shadow 1.42, flat+shadow 0.28 / 0.94, flat no shadow 0.95. Textures cost about +0.6-0.8 ms of main, shadows 0.25-0.4 ms.
  • Normal/specular strength. pbr.DefaultNormalStrength 2.2 scales the tangent xy of the decoded normal (pbr.DecodeNormalK, twin in voxel.frag via S.view.w), pbr.DefaultSpecBoost 2.0 scales sun GGX and environment reflection (S.ambient.w). Flags -normal-strength, -spec-boost (1 = plain LabPBR), RenderPlugin.NormalStrength/SpecBoost, Renderer.SetPBRStrength; F6 cycles default / plain / strong when window.Window has PBRPressed() (not wired yet, see report).
  • Parallax relief. The alpha of the normal map is a LabPBR height (255 = top surface, lower = deeper). On faces within about 24 blocks (fading from 10) pomUV() in voxel_body.glsl marches the view ray through that height field (steep parallax, up to pbr.DefaultParallaxSteps 28 layers, more at grazing angles, refined by a 4-step binary search then a linear interpolation between the two bracketing samples) and every texture fetch uses the found texel with explicit gradients; crevices it finds are darkened a little, and pomShadow() marches 8 steps from the found point toward the sun so raised texels cast soft self-shadows. Depth is pbr.DefaultParallaxDepth 0.07 blocks for the full height range (UBO S.fx.zw); flag -parallax N (negative = off), vulkan.Options.ParallaxDepth, Renderer.SetParallax, editor Preferences > Rendering. Foliage and glass do not use it, and the soft renderer ignores it (GPU only, like the wet surfaces). Most bundled textures ship a flat height, so BuildLayers derives one from the albedo (blocktex.AutoHeight: dark = deep crevices and mortar, tileable; textures that already have a height keep it; when the normal map is missing or flat too, NormalFromHeight shades that relief; blocktex.AutoRelief = false turns that off). The F6 "plain" preset also switches parallax off.
  • Wet surfaces and porosity. In the HDR levels rain darkens and glosses open faces; the darkening, the gloss of the film and the puddle coverage now follow the LabPBR porosity of the specular map (B 0..64; textures that store none are judged from their roughness): porous stone, dirt and wood darken most, hard polished surfaces stay bright and glossy. Water itself keeps the SSR / Fresnel sky reflection of water.frag.
  • Making relief for your own textures. The texture editor's Relief maps row derives a height from the colours, a normal map from it and a constant specular map, previews the normal map and writes <name>_n.png / <name>_s.png (LabPBR names) next to the texture, so a project texture gets normal, height and specular like the bundled ones (Editor.TexGenerateRelief, blocktex.AutoHeight / NormalFromHeight / GenerateSpec).
  • Biome tint. worldgen.ClimateAt(seed, x, z) (smooth value noise, 192/64 block wavelengths, 1/256 quantised) feeds blocktex.BiomeColor (bilinear blend of cold/dry, cold/wet, hot/dry, hot/wet colormap corners). The mesher (mesh.Opts.Biome, mesh.BiomeProvider) gives each tinted face the tint of its block column (such faces are not merged); grass-side overlays baked with the plains tint get Vertex.Shift (ratio biome/plains, applied to green texels only). Soft uses the same function (render.Options.TintFn). -biome-tint=false / VoxelWorldPlugin.NoBiomeTint restores the plains tint. Tint is per block column (not per vertex): the field changes < 1/16 per block, so no seams.
  • Water. mesh.Vertex.Depth = liquid blocks below a surface (max 8, part of the greedy merge key). water.frag derives opacity from the depth along the view path, darker/bluer deep colour, a finer second ripple octave and tick-driven foam streaks on one-block-deep water; everything is a function of the sim tick only. Water has no texture in the pack (flat colour). In the natural/vivid looks (not classic) the colour leans blue-green, shallow water is a light turquoise band that darkens and cools with the column depth, Fresnel reflects the sky, ripples are stronger with slope-based light variation and the foam is thin streaks; alpha is capped at 0.88. Still no depth-buffer refraction or true shore distance.
  • Foliage. In the natural/vivid looks a tinted layer whose top mip alpha is < 1 is treated as leaves (leafF in voxel_body.glsl, twin in render/soft/tex.go): darkest texels lifted, per-block hue variation from the tone hash (do NOT add a second hash3 call there: MoltenVK miscompiled it into speckle), soft AO, ambient floor, light leaking through the canopy, no specular, and alpha test with mip-level coverage compensation. Classic look is untouched.
  • Glass. Where a pack ships no glass albedo, blocktex synthesises a cut-out albedo (frame + glints) so the pane is see-through with shaded frame. A tinted translucent glass would need a blended pass for non-liquid blocks; not done.
  • Slice view. Renderer.SetSlice(maxY, on) / RenderPlugin.SetSliceMaxY(y, on) / -slice-y N: chunks wholly above are skipped, the cut chunk row is re-meshed with the world above treated as air (real cap faces, VoxelWorld.SetSlice), shaders also discard above the plane (S.slice), shadows use the clipping pipeline, soft treats y > N as air.
  • Texture filter. -texfilter linear is now the default (trilinear minification, nearest magnification); consecutive frames 0.3 block apart: far-band mean diff 3.74 -> 3.23, near band 19.2 -> 15.9 (nearest -> linear). Dark far dirt ridges are lighting (back-facing risers get ambient only), BURROW_TEXDEBUG=albedo shows plain brown dirt there.
  • Animation. Not implemented: lava_still, fire_0/1, magma, sea_lantern, prismarine are animated strips but the engine registers none of these blocks, so water/glass stay static. See CHANGELOG.

Looks and colour pipeline (stage L1)#

-look natural|vivid|classic (F7 cycles) selects a render.Look parameter bundle; -exposure, -saturation, -contrast and -tonemap none|aces|filmic override single values (0/empty = preset). Render settings never touch the simulation hash.

lookpipelineexposuresaturationcontrasttonemaptint gainsun / ambient gainfog startnormal / spec
natural (default)linear HDR1.050.861.04aces1.120.5 / 0.70.45 far1.2 / 0.3
vividlinear HDR1.01.051.08aces1.120.55 / 0.70.45 far1.2 / 0.3

Linear looks: albedo is decoded with the exact sRGB curve (tint and tone are applied in sRGB as before, NaturalTint caps red at 0.72 green and softens blue so hot biomes lean olive), lit in linear HDR: albedo * (hemi * ao + sun * N.L * shadow * (0.6 + 0.4 ao)) with hemi = mix(ground, sky, n.y/2+0.5) * faceK (top 1, +-z 0.92, +-x 0.84, bottom 0.72), plus GGX sun specular (roughness floor 0.3), sky reflection (x0.4) and emission (x2). The result goes through grade() (exposure, tone map, sRGB encode, saturation, contrast), fog mixes in display space with the fog colour (the horizon, or env.fog when set, for the terrain, water, particles and the classic scene meshes (sceneFogColor() in shaders/scene3d.glsl): a separate display-space colour in the camera basis w slots, fogColor() in shaders/common.glsl; the HDR levels use fogSky()), and the shader writes srgbDecode(display) so the sRGB target re-encodes exactly. Sky/fog colours come from scene.Sky.ColorsNatural, lights from scene.Sky.HDR (noon sun 3.0 neutral-warm, orange and dimmer when low, cool moonlit ambient at night, warm ambient at twilight, continuous in time). The soft renderer implements the same formulas (render.Look.Grade, soft.shadeTexLinear). The classic look compiles to the original shaders (voxel.frag, glass.frag); natural and vivid use voxel_lin.frag/glass_lin.frag (separate pipelines). Mesh/sprite scene3d shaders keep the display-space lighting.

The classic and linear pipelines take env.sky as display-space colours (gamma-encoded through outLin, so a linear target shows them as written) and keep env.fog apart from the horizon: the sky gradient is the horizon, the terrain fades into the fog colour. render/soft follows: the same split, the night stars of env.sky (a hash-based point field above the horizon that fades in with the sun below -0.05 and is off with stars: off) and a cheap bloom for env.post bloom > 0 (pixels over 80% luminance blurred twice with a box and added back); both are only drawn when asked, so the default golden images do not change.

Sky ticks (-tick N, N=0 means the default 9000): 300 sunrise, 6000 noon, 11500 low sun, 12000 sunset, 18000 night. Measure a screenshot with go run ./cmd/lookmeasure a.png b.png. Natural-vs-classic noon (open grassland, same camera): mean luma 143 -> 121, clipped pixels 11.8 % -> 0.0 %, highlight fraction 32 % -> 8 %, grass RGB (160,205,112) -> (127,168,86), lit grass luma 223 -> 174.

Target selection indicator#

The block under the crosshair (game, burrowplay, editor viewport hover) is marked by a thin soft outline that follows the block's real selection shape: voxel.SelectionBoxes resolves the boxes of the shape model (slabs, stairs, fences, walls, doors, trapdoors, torches, cross plants, cactus...), app.FrameState.TargetBoxes carries them (nil = the unit cell of a cube) and render.SelectionSegments reduces them to the edges of their union: the parts of an edge buried inside another box and duplicate edges are dropped, the outer edges are pushed out by 0.003 block.

  • Shaders sel.vert/sel.frag: one screen-space quad per segment, about 1.75 px wide at 1080p (scales with the render height), coverage falls off over one pixel at the sides and at the ends (no stair-stepping). Near-black (0.04, 0.04, 0.05), alpha 0.7. Drawn in the scene pass of every shader level (off/classic, low to ultra), before the temporal resolve; a near-black line does not bloom and moving it between blocks leaves no TAA / FSR 2 trail.
  • Depth: tested against the scene with a world-space pull toward the camera (0.004 block + 0.07 % of the distance), so far blocks neither flicker nor z-fight.
  • Optional ghost (Ghost, off by default): the hidden part of the outline, drawn with a GREATER depth test at the given alpha (about 0.12 looks right).
  • Hit-face accent (Face, default 0.10): selface.* lifts the face under the crosshair (TargetNormal) by 10 %, blended as dst * (1 + k) so it reads the same on sunlit and shaded surfaces (a white overlay washed out shaded cross plants in the linear HDR chain).
  • No breathing animation and no mining progress (the engine has no break progress); the indicator is static, so -shot captures stay deterministic.

Configure with engine.RenderSettings.Selection (a *render.SelectionStyle: Color, Alpha, Width multiplier, Ghost, Face; nil = render.DefaultSelection) or vulkan.Renderer.SetSelectionStyle. To see it: voxel_sandbox -shapes -gallery-shapes -shot out.png -freeze -cam 1.5,41.9,4.5,0,-25.

Graphics settings, changed at run time#

A shader level is a bundle. Every part of it is also a setting of its own, so a game can keep high and turn bloom off, or keep medium and add 32 god ray samples. Settings sit on top of the level's preset; a level change keeps them. Render only: nothing here touches the simulation or StateHash.

  • Project: runtime.shaders (the level at start; -shaders and BURROW_SHADERS win) and runtime.graphics, for example {"bloom": false, "god_rays": 32} (a switch is true/false or 0/1). burrow config set runtime.graphics '{"ao": false}'.
  • Script: burrowsdk.SetShaders("high"), SetGraphics("god_rays", 32), SetGraphicsOn("bloom", false) and ResetGraphics() (frame domain, from the next frame, ignored by headless runs and servers; an unknown name or an out of range value returns INVALID).
  • Editor: the viewport View menu switches the level; the MCP tool editor_viewport_screenshot takes shaders.
  • Go: RenderSettings.Shaders and RenderSettings.Graphics of the engine, applied at the next frame.

A change rebuilds the post pipeline: expect one frame hitch (the GPU is drained first). Turning the level to off drops the HDR pipeline, and the settings then have nothing to adjust until the level is raised again; they are kept. The console prints shaders: and graphics: (what differs from the preset) after each change.

Shader levels, stage S2: hybrid / editor / burrowplay, block light, AO, shadows#

  • HDR everywhere the terrain is drawn. The HDR target, atmosphere and post chain now also run in hybrid frames (SetHybrid: burrowplay and games with scene components), in RenderHybridToTarget (the burrowedit viewport and editor screenshots) and therefore with -shaders in burrowedit. The post resources are per target (postState copy sharing the pipelines: own HDR image, bloom chain, 1x1 exposure history) so the editor viewport adapts its exposure on its own; a fresh target (screenshots) starts with the exact exposure of its first frame. Scene meshes use shaders/mesh_hdr.frag (linear albedo, the atmosphere light, shadow map, aerial perspective; point lights and emissive as before), sprites output linear, custom user shaders keep their own (display-space, written as linear) output. The canvas layer of a hybrid frame is drawn after the tone mapping, in the composite pass (no depth attachment). Frames with a scene but no terrain (RenderFrame without SetHybrid) keep the off pipeline. -shaders off and -look classic are bit-identical to before (the parity runs check it).
  • GPU time per pass. Offscreen frames carry timestamps too (read right after the synchronous submit, never from a target's first frame). The editor Profiler lists Shadow, Sky (collapsed main pass on Metal), ... and every post pass (post ao, post bloom-prefilter, ..., post composite).
  • Block light (render only, never in the sim or StateHash). voxel.Block.Emit (0..15, JSON emit, omitted when 0) or a well-known name (glowstone, lava, torch, lantern, sea_lantern, shroomlight, jack_o_lantern, redstone_lamp, campfire, fire, magma_block, soul_torch; Block.Emission) makes a block a coloured source. mesh.Opts.BlockLight (set by the renderer for the HDR levels only) makes the mesher flood-fill the sources of a 64^3 window around the chunk through every non-opaque block, one level lost per block and per colour channel (a warm light turns redder with distance), and store the light per vertex (Vertex.Light, 4/4/3 bits packed above the AO bits of the vertex word; the vertex stays 16 bytes). Corners average the open cells around them (smooth lighting); quads merge only when their light is uniform and equal, so unlit terrain meshes exactly as before (and a registry without emitters costs nothing). An edit with block light on re-meshes the 26 neighbour chunks instead of 6. Light crosses chunk borders (tested seam-free). The shader adds 2.6 * light^2.2 times the albedo and AO. Superseded by lighting.md: the mesher now reads a persistent voxel.LightField (block light plus skylight, per-block attenuation, incremental updates, all shader levels, soft parity); the window flood fill above remains as the fallback of Opts.BlockLight without a field.
  • Ambient occlusion (medium+; shaders/post_ao.frag, post_aoblur.frag): horizon-based AO on the depth buffer written by the main pass (a ground-truth-AO style slice integration written from the published formulation), half resolution, interleaved-gradient jitter, 5x5 depth-aware blur, multiplied into the lit colour in the composite. Slices x steps: medium 2x5, high 3x6, ultra 4x8; radius 1.6-2 blocks. It adds to the per-vertex voxel AO.
  • Sun shadow filter. Shader levels use a rotated Vogel disk of 8 (low) / 12 / 16 / 24 taps over the hardware compare taps with a per-pixel rotation, a radius of 1.6-2.8 shadow texels and a slope-scaled normal offset and depth bias that grow with the radius (no acne at grazing sun); the moon casts the same way at night. Classic levels keep the 3x3 box. A real PCSS needs the raw depth of the shadow map next to the compare sampler (not done).
  • Look tuning against the target palette (local comparison only, nothing copied): sunset/sunrise sky glow is orange toward the sun and lilac above (atmo.glsl warm/blue-hour terms and the CPU twin), golden-hour sun light is stronger and warmer, clouds pick up the colour, haze is lighter (aerial density 0.0007, cap 0.34), the composite is more saturated (x1.26) with a gentler highlight roll-off (sand keeps its colour), moonlight and the night floor are brighter (blue-night, no black water), the moon disc is no longer burnt, water at dusk keeps a blue-green body and a pink-grey reflection instead of violet, and the foam streaks are broken up by a slow mask and fade with distance (no regular white dots; sun glints are capped and faded).
  • Budget (M2 Max, 2560x1440, radius 12, shadows on, camera 0,58,100 up 8 degrees, validation on, GPU ms): off total 3.2; low 4.3 (post 0.6); medium 5.5 (post 1.0, AO 0.07 + blur 0.27, composite 0.30); high 5.8 (post 0.8); ultra 8.4 (post 0.8). All are far below 16.6 ms (the sky/cloud pass is most of the growth). The editor viewport (1194x522, hybrid render + synchronous readback, selftest): 5.2 ms off, 5.4 medium, 5.7 ultra per frame; UI 0.55 ms.
  • Not in S2: SSR / water reflections, refraction, caustics, god rays, DoF, motion blur (stage S3, below); TAA and GI.

  • Water mesh. With an HDR level, VoxelWorld.SetWaterSurface makes the mesher leave the liquid blocks out of the cube mesh (mesh.Opts.LiquidSurface) and build Result.Liquid (mesh.BuildLiquidSurface): top faces with the corner heights of LiquidCornerHeights (flowing water slopes, no full cubes, no faces between two liquid cells), sides only toward open cells, bottoms over air. The fine water bricks (mesh.Opts.FineWater, exported once per tick from the fluid state and re-meshed at most every 4 ticks per chunk) are appended; both use the mesh.FineVertex layout packed to 24 bytes (waterStride, pipeline water2.vert). On a block corner both meshes take the same height, so there is no crack. Falling fine water is carried by the automaton's streams (a debit per sub-column, no bricks in the air) and drawn from them (mesh/cascade.go, see water-fine.md "Rendering"): a translucent curtain leaving the lip along the free-fall parabola, joined across lips, tapered and wobbling at its open ends, whose opacity, aeration and streak speed follow the debit and the fall (thin = glassy with the cliff refracted behind, thick = white), plus at the foot GPU-animated spray droplets, churning boils, rising mist and expanding foam rings on the water. Moving fine water re-meshes only the water of its chunks (Mesher.BuildWater on the workers, UploadChunkWaterOnly), and far chunks (> 96 blocks) draw their bricks as the coarse surface.
  • Scene copy. When water is in view the HDR main pass is split: after the opaque terrain, sky and highlight, a full-screen pass copies the lit colour and the linear depth (alpha) into scene-copy (RGBA16F), then the pass resumes (load ops) for the water, glass, rain and hybrid transparents. The water writes alpha 1 and does its own compositing.
  • Water shader (water_hdr.frag, written from published descriptions, nothing copied): wave normals from octaves of travelling directional waves around the wind (deep-water dispersion), a slow domain warp, cross-current ripples along LiquidFlow / the fine Flow; amplitude from the wind gust (calm water is nearly a mirror). Schlick Fresnel (F0 0.02). Reflection: screen-space ray march against the scene copy with a binary refinement and edge / distance fades, falling back to the sky model, a one-tap cloud layer and a normalised sun / moon glint. Refraction: the scene copy offset by the normal, Beer-Lambert absorption over the real path to the bottom (screen depth), in-scattering lit by the sky and the sun (shadowed). Caustics (high+): an animated ridged-noise pattern on the bottom, fading with depth. Foam: shore (the depth difference, i.e. the real distance to the bottom), current speed, the fine-water foam attribute and falling sheets (the shore fringe is subtle). Seen from below: Snell's window with total internal reflection, then the under-water fog. Look, after the Minecraft shader packs: the absorption is tinted by the biome / registry colour (low saturation: a swamp goes green, the default stays clear; red is absorbed first), the in-scattering is a teal in the shallows and a deep blue in the depth, the sky mirrored in the water is occluded under cliffs and canopies (skyVis), and the sun glint is GGX (sharp when calm, long tails in a breeze, shadowed by the sun shadow map). water.glsl (low / classic path) shares the palette and the sky occlusion. The classic render/soft renderer applies the same per-channel depth absorption (deep water is darker and bluer).
  • Water level. Like vanilla, a free liquid surface stands below the block top: voxel.LiquidSurfaceFrac = 8/9 for a source, level/8 * 8/9 for flowing levels; a cell with liquid directly above stays full (1), so columns are continuous. LiquidTable.SurfaceHeight, SurfaceCorners (mesh.LiquidCornerHeights), BlockCornerHeight and LocalSurfaceHeight are the drawn surface: the coarse mesh, the fine-water bricks (column scales a free column the same way, so both meet at a block corner), the terrace drop (to the lower surface, one block down minus 1/9) and the cascade lip / impact read them, and engine.HeadUnderWater compares the eye with it. Height, Submerged, LocalHeight, Sample (swimming, buoyancy, the simulation and its hash) keep the full cell. The classic cube water keeps its own 0.06..0.1 dip (water.vert).
  • Under water. FrameState.HeadUnder (or, for free cameras and the editor, the eye inside a liquid surface: engine.HeadUnderWater) fades every HDR surface and the sky into the water colour (underwaterFog, per-channel extinction).
  • Weather (render.WeatherFX, filled by the weather plugin once per frame from weather.Res and State.At(camera), weather.RenderFX): cloud cover from State.Cover when -clouds is auto (the flag keeps priority), overcast clouds with fewer march steps, the sun scaled by SkyLightFactor, fog / humidity / precipitation thicken the aerial perspective, the sky follows the weather clock (scene.NewSky(Clock)), cloud drift integrates the wind. Rain and snow: instanced camera-relative streaks / flakes (precip.vert, no vertex buffer, at most Precip instances scaled by the intensity), slanted by the wind, removed under a roof, a canopy or in a cave by the precipitation height map (64 m around the camera at every voxel size: 64 x 64 columns at 1 voxel per metre, up to 256 x 256 texels of several voxels, top of the highest solid or opaque block, engine.PrecipMapUnit, Renderer.SetPrecipMapGrid, set 2 binding 3). Lightning: FlashAt adds a cold fill light to the ambient and the sky; a jagged bolt is drawn in the sky at the strike bearing. Wet surfaces (Wetness, open faces only by the same height map): darker porous albedo, glossier, puddles on flat tops (noise mask) that mirror the sky, raindrop rings while it rains. Wind sway: leaves and cross / tall plants (vulkan.Options.Sway, flag in the tint byte, anchored at the ground for plants), deterministic in the frame tick.
  • Atmosphere. Sun shafts (high+): half-resolution radial samples toward the sun over the sky mask of the depth buffer, added in the composite with the sun colour. DoF + motion blur (ultra only): one full-resolution pass, a 12-tap disk for the far field beyond 1.6x the focus distance (screen centre) and camera motion blur by reprojection with the previous view-projection (8 taps). TAA (not part of S3, added later, see anti-aliasing.md): the SSR uses no per-frame jitter (fixed steps plus a binary refinement, faded at the edges), the AO is already blurred, and the remaining shimmer (far ripples, rain streaks) fades with distance; a history buffer would cost a pass and ghost the rain for no visible gain.

The line of small blue blocks across the river (img/worldgen-meadow-shapes.png) and the dark horizontal bands (img/worldgen-river.png) are generator terraces: worldgen/terrain2.go sets the river level per column to floor(sea + (hb - sea) / 2), so the level drops by one block along level lines of the base height (65 steps in x 0..140, z -200..0 of seed 1). The base water never flows, so a one-block wall of source water stands across the river; the old cube water drew its side faces (and faces between different liquid ids) as translucent walls.   • Swell. With a swell (runtime.plugins.swell, package swell, physics.md) the smooth water surface rides three travelling waves: water2.vert lifts the top vertices and the upper edge of the sides (the pack marks them with the alpha of the vertex colour, waveWeights; a zero-depth corner of a shore stays put), water_hdr.frag and water.glsl tilt the normal by the slope of the swell, and the eye under the surface follows it. The scene UBO carries the amplitude, wavelength, heading and the time phase of each wave in floats 224..231 (S.swellA, S.swellB, shaders/swell.glsl). The classic cube water does not move (its greedy quads would crack). BURROW_SWELL=amp,length,dir forces a swell for captures.

Flowing water (water_1..water_7, water-physics.md) is ordinary blocks; mesh.LiquidHeight/LiquidCornerHeights/LiquidFlow give a renderer the surface height, smooth corner heights and flow direction of a water cell.

Environment overrides of the sky (scene.SkyLook)#

The environment components (env.sky, env.sun, env.fog, env.post; fields and blending in lighting.md) reach the renderers as engine.EnvState. The part that overrides what the clock computes is a renderer-neutral scene.SkyLook (zenith, horizon and fog colours, sun tint, fixed sun direction, stars off, bloom offset): vulkan.Renderer.SetEnvLook and soft.Renderer.Env take it, the zero value changes nothing. The grade (exposure, saturation, contrast, sun gain), clouds, shadows and fog density go through SetLook, SetCloudCover, SetShadows and WeatherFX.Fog as before. engine.envApplier (game) and engine.EnvApplier (editor viewport) push it once per change.

  • Bloom: the post pass multiplies its strength by 1 + bloom (levels with bloom only).
  • Stars: the star visibility of the atmosphere is zeroed.
  • Sun tint and fixed direction: applied to the light the frame computes (SkyLook.Light, SkyLook.Atmo); a fixed direction replaces the clock sky by the one whose sun stands at the same elevation (SkyLook.Sky) so transmittance, twilight and ambient fit it.
  • Sky and fog colours at the HDR levels: the procedural atmosphere (shaders/atmo.glsl) mixes atmoSky toward the given colours (display values, converted to linear radiance with gain 1.6) with the weights carried in the w of the horizon and zenith vectors of the scene uniform, and fogSky returns the fog colour carried in the w of camFwd, camRight and camUp (all zero = the sky). These components were unused; the uniform layout is unchanged. At the classic level the colours replace the sky gradient ends and the fog fades to the (fog or horizon) colour.
  • Tests: scene (TestSkyLookFixedSun), render/soft (TestEnvLookChangesImage), and render/vulkan envLookChecks in the GPU suite (BURROW_GPU=1, BURROW_GPU_ONLY=env for just these): each field must move the image, and resetting the look must bring the base image back.

Air distortion#

Heat haze above lava, fire and torches, shimmering volumes and shock waves, on the HDR levels medium and up (render.ShaderQuality.Distortion; off at low, in the classic pipeline and in the soft renderer, which ignores it). Frame domain only: nothing here reads or writes simulation state, the StateHash of a run is the same with and without the effect (the one exception is registering the fx.distortion component, which is opt-in like every component, see below).

  • Passes (render/vulkan/distort.go, shaders distort*.vert/frag, particle_dist.frag). When a frame has sources, three post passes run right after the main pass, before anything else samples the HDR colour (TAA / FSR 2, DoF and motion blur, bloom, exposure, tone mapping): distort-map draws every source into a half-resolution RGBA16F buffer as a screen-space UV offset (one instanced soft volume per source, additive blending, fragments behind opaque geometry dropped by sampling the main depth); distort-apply offsets the HDR colour by that buffer into a scratch image; distort-copy copies it back, alpha kept. No source in view: no pass runs and nothing is drawn. The three passes appear by name in GPUTimes.Post (vulkan.Stats().GPU.Post), so they are in the per-pass GPU timings of the perf stats; the buffers are a half-resolution and a full-resolution RGBA16F image (about 20 MB at 1080p), allocated only at the levels that have the effect.
  • Depth aware. distort-apply compares the depth of the pixel with the depth of the offset sample and fades the offset out when the sample is nearer (up to 0.6 blocks): a foreground object is never smeared into the background. The offset fades to zero within 5% of the screen edges (no samples from outside the image), and every source fades with distance (full near, gone past 112 blocks).
  • Water. When the scene copy was taken this frame (water, glass or precipitation in view), pixels where water or glass lies in front of the opaque scene (the copy's depth differs from the depth buffer) are left alone: the water already refracts, no double distortion.
  • Sources. (a) Hot blocks: voxel.Block.HeatLevel() (0..15; the block's heat field, else a default by name: lava 15, fire 12, campfire 10, magma 5, lit furnaces 6-8, torches 3). distortion.HeatScanner scans the loaded chunks within 3 chunks of the camera once per chunk version (the same cheap Chunk.Each scan as the torch fire effects), merges the hot cells into 4x8x4 buckets per chunk and HeatSources turns the nearest 48 into rising columns (height 1.2 to 5 blocks, strength 0.3 to 1.2 by heat). The columns are owned by an fx.heat entity (scene.FxHeat: strength multiplier, disabled; the Environment node of new projects carries one, found by a scene tree scan): none, no heat. runtime.distortion stays the global quality switch and multiplier. Projects whose scenes never mention fx.heat (and packed games) keep the always-on heat until a scene gets an fx.heat entity. (b) the fx.distortion scene component. (c) particle emitters with distortion (particles.md). (d) shock waves: expanding rings emitted by an entity (fx_shockwave(entity, offset...), centred on its position plus the offset; a wave from the simulation without an entity is dropped; distplug.Distortion.Shockwave is the host-code form with an absolute position), at most 16 alive. At most render.MaxDistortionSources (128) volumes per frame.
  • fx.distortion (distortion.Component): shape (sphere, box), size (diameter or box sides, blocks), strength (1 is up to about 14 px at 1080p near the camera), speed, scale (ripple frequency), rise (how fast the pattern travels up; heat uses 1), disabled. Zero values mean the default (strength, speed, scale 1; size 2). It is schema-driven, so the inspector edits it, editor_set_component / entity_get_component and the Go SDK accessors (FxDistortionStrength, ...) work. It changes the registry hash, so it is registered only when runtime.plugins.distortion is true or a scene or prefab of the project mentions it (the editor always registers it); projects that never use it keep their hashes.
  • Settings. runtime.distortion in vox.project: true / "on" (default), false / "off", or a strength multiplier 0..4 (JSON number or string). burrowplay -distortion off|on|<strength> overrides it. runtime.plugins.distortion: false removes the plugin altogether.
  • Limits. The CPU renderer (render/soft, headless screenshots) draws no distortion (distortion-only particles are skipped). The offset is not written to the TAA velocity buffer, so under heavy TAA / FSR 2 the shimmer is smoothed by the temporal history; translucent glass drawn before the passes is distorted with the rest of the frame. Heat columns are anchored to the top of the hottest cell of a bucket (lava lakes merge into 4 x 4 blocks patches); a lit furnace needs a block whose name or heat says so (the engine has no furnace state).

Anti-aliasing and upscaling#

FXAA, SMAA 1x, TAA (Halton jitter, velocity buffer, YCoCg variance clipping, reactive mask), FSR 1 and FSR 2 on the HDR levels, -aa off|fxaa|smaa|taa, -upscale off|fsr1|fsr2, -render-scale native|quality|balanced|performance|0.5..1, a pluggable vulkan.Upscaler (DLSS and XeSS stubs: their SDKs are not bundled), HUD composited at native resolution after the upscale, no-op on the soft backend. The shader level picks the default (low fxaa, medium smaa, high and ultra taa). Everything is in anti-aliasing.md.

Materials and custom shaders#

A draw with Material.Shader set uses a user vertex/fragment pair on the Scene3D contract (shaders/scene3d.glsl): sets 0 (scene), 1 (texture slot 0), 2 (frame) as for the built-in meshes, plus set 3 (render/vulkan/matblock.go): bindings 0..2 are the textures of material slots 1..3 and binding 3 is a 64-byte dynamic uniform with the 16 material floats of the draw. rworld.Material.Ext carries floats 4..15 and the slot 1..3 textures from a resolved material asset (shadersys.Resolve). Pipelines of built-in modules are rebuilt when a project shader override is applied or removed (render/vulkan/shaderreload.go, also the anti-aliasing, upscaling, particle and ray-tracing passes); a material shader's pipelines follow its asset version, and a material with a displacing vertex stage or a discard also draws with its own stages in the shadow and velocity passes. See materials.md and assets.md.

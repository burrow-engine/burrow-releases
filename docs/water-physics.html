Water physics#

Water flows, pushes and carries. Three layers, each deterministic and cheap when nothing moves:

layerpackagedomainwhat it does
flowfluidsimulation (20 Hz)cellular automaton on the voxel grid: sources, levels, fall, spread, drain, lava
interactionphysics, appsimulation / frameimmersion, Archimedes' push, drag, currents, swimming, climbing out
data for renderingvoxel, meshread onlysurface height of a water cell, smooth corner heights, flow direction

Rendering itself (refraction, foam, caustics, underwater fog) is a separate stage and is not part of this page; it reads the data listed in For the renderer.

This page describes the coarse automaton (blocks water, water_1..7), kept for runtime.plugins.fine_water: false. The fine water (the default of the fluid plugin, fluid.Config.Fine) replaces it with a conserved volume in sub-cell bricks where water moves: see water-fine.md.

Data model: levels are blocks#

The water of the world is voxels. There is no side table of "water levels":

blockmeaning
watersource: a full cell, never changes by itself (the worldgen sea is made of sources)
water_7 ... water_1flowing water, height level/8 of the cell (7 = highest)

fluid.Install (called by fluid.Plugin) registers water_1 .. water_7 after the terrain blocks (voxel.Block.Liquid = level; a block of Fluid kind water without Liquid is level 8; the name never matters: a custom block with "fluid": "water" flows). Ids are appended, so existing ids, worlds and .voxworld files stay valid (a file that lists fewer blocks than the registry loads).

Why blocks and not metadata or a sidecar:

  • Overlay and StateHash. Flowing water is written like any edit: it lives in the sparse overlay, in Chunk.Hash, in snapshots, .voxworld, undo and the editor's DocHash, with no new storage and no new serialization. A sidecar would need its own hash, snapshot, undo and world-file story, and could disagree with the voxels.
  • Pure base. The sea stays a field of sources in the base terrain. It never moves by itself; only cells next to an edit (the overlay) are ever evaluated. Digging next to the sea clones the base chunk into the overlay like any edit.
  • Rendering and tools see ordinary blocks: the mesher merges per block id, palettes and the place tool work unchanged.
  • Cost: 8 ids instead of a per-voxel byte. A chunk with water has a palette of at most 9 water entries.

The only extra state is the work list (fluid.State, a sim.Extra named fluid): the cells that may be unstable plus the wake-ups not merged yet. It is derived from the voxels (a cell that is not in it is stable; TestWorkListIsDerivable rebuilds it by sweeping the overlay and reaches the same settled world), so:

  • it is serialized in Snapshot (a restore taken in the middle of a flow resumes exactly), rolled back with a failed tick,
  • it is not in StateHash: "place a source then undo" leaves the same hash as before, and a world without moving water has the same hash with or without the plugin. Only a non-default fluid.Config and the count of shed cells are hashed.

Rules (one automaton step)#

A step runs every Interval ticks (default 5: water advances 4 cells per second). Each candidate cell is evaluated against the unchanged world (two phases, so order cannot matter), then the changes are applied in canonical order.

  • Solid cells, bounds and sources never change. A free cell is air, flowing water, a fragile block, or lava.
  • Fall. Water directly above makes the cell falling water (water_7).
  • Spread. Otherwise the cell takes the highest spread of its four side neighbours: a source spreads 7, a falling cell 7, a flowing cell of level k spreads k-1. A flowing or falling cell only spreads sideways when it rests on a solid block (otherwise it falls); a source always does. Range from a source: 7 cells.
  • Infinite sources. A free cell with two side neighbours that are sources, over a solid or source block, becomes a source (Config.NoInfiniteSources turns this off).
  • Drain. Unfed flowing water becomes air; a puddle recedes one cell per step from its edge.
  • Fragile blocks. voxel.Block.Fragile (non-solid: plants, torches) are replaced by flowing water. No default block is fragile. Other non-solid blocks (a pane, a sign) block the flow like walls.
  • Lava. A block of Fluid kind lava reached by water becomes the block named by Config.CoolSource (default obsidian), flowing lava CoolFlow (cobblestone), water met by lava CoolWater (stone); a name that is not a registered solid block means no conversion and lava simply blocks water. Without Config.Lava lava does not flow itself (see "Flowing lava").
  • Bounds. Config.Min/Max (default x,z +-2^20, y -128..512) delimit the world the automaton may touch. Outside is the void: never written and not solid, so a stream at the edge falls out of the world.
  • Streaming and the void. The simulation world is total (pure base terrain plus overlay): there is no "unloaded chunk" in the sim, and streaming never changes results, so water flows into terrain that is not meshed yet exactly as into meshed terrain. What is bounded is the work (below) and the box (above).

Waking: no dedicated command needed#

A voxel.Observer on the chunk map reports every effective voxel write and every SetChunk. A written voxel wakes 15 cells (the cell and the cells whose result depends on it). So placing or removing a source, breaking a dam, undo, scripts, fill_box and the automaton's own writes all start the flow. fluid_wake {min,max} queues a scan of the chunks of a box (use it for a world saved mid-flow; world loads already queue their chunks).

Writes may arrive in any order (a command looping over a Go map): nothing depends on it. Below 8192 queued writes the cells are a multiset consumed sorted; at the first write past it the queue becomes the sorted set of the chunks of all the writes (later writes add their chunk), so the outcome is the union of the chunks whatever the arrival order. A chunk scan covers a chunk that holds water or touches (6 neighbours, base terrain included) a chunk that does. More than 4096 distinct queued chunks start a sweep of the overlay chunks (128 examined per step); the cell wake-ups are kept, and a chunk the cursor has not reached is covered by the sweep itself. SetChunk (world load, agent history) queues a chunk only if it, its predecessor or a neighbour holds water.

Cost and denial of service#

Per step, Config bounds the work:

capdefaultmeaning
MaxUpdates2048cells changed; an ocean breach or a dam failure spreads over many steps
MaxActive262144unstable cells waiting for MaxUpdates room (3 MiB); beyond it the highest cells are shed and counted (Dropped, hashed)
ChunkScans2whole-chunk scans per step (32768 evaluations each)

Every step evaluates all candidates (the work list plus 15 cells per queued write, at most 15 x 8192 in a burst; the threshold was 65536 before the fine-water stage, when one burst of scattered writes could cost a 257 ms step, now 30 ms at worst), so stale wake-ups cost time but never a budget and the future depends only on the voxels. Lower cells are served first, so a falling stream is not starved by its sides. Idle cost is a branch (12.9 us per tick including Step overhead). Measured on an M2 Max (go test ./fluid -bench . -run X, TestOceanCostBounded):

  • sea of 66k source cells with its east wall removed: 82 steps, 28520 cells changed, at most 1132 per step, work list peak 95k cells (1.1 MiB), 0.12 ms per tick on average, worst tick 12 ms (the first merge of 65k wake-ups);
  • a one-column breach in a 29x5x59 reservoir with MaxUpdates 64: never more than 64 changes per step.

Memory growth is bounded by the caps, the 8192-entry wake queue, the 4096-entry chunk queue and the world box. A flood can still create overlay chunks as far as water can legitimately reach (range 7 from a source, unbounded only through infinite sources filling a basin that is open to a source).

Determinism contract#

Same seed, same journal, same config: same StateHash. Everything in fluid is integer arithmetic over sorted slices; no map iteration, no float, no goroutine. Tests (go test ./fluid ./physics ./editor/core): two runs, journal replay, snapshot/restore in the middle of a flow (and into the same engine, which re-attaches the observer), rollback of a failed tick, write-order independence, queue overflow, dam break and ocean bounds, 6 fuzz seeds, derivability of the work list, engine.VerifyCacheNeutrality with water and bodies.

Interaction (physics)#

physics.PhysicsPlugin reads liquids from the registry (voxel.LiquidTable); a world without liquid blocks pays nothing and physics.Config.Water.Off ignores them. Tuning: physics.WaterConfig.

  • Immersion (LiquidTable.Sample): the exact volume of the box under the liquid surfaces it overlaps, a fraction 0..1, plus the volume-weighted current. Query.Immersion(entity) and Query.WaterAt(box) expose it to frame and fixed code.
  • Dynamic bodies: Archimedes' push Density * submergedVolume / mass against the scaled gravity (a body floats when its density Mass / (8 hx hy hz) is below WaterConfig.Density, default 1), and drag toward the current speed (FlowSpeed * flow direction, default 1.6 m/s). Semi-implicit drag: a floating body settles instead of ringing (TestBodyFloatsAndSettles: bobbing under 5 mm).
  • Characters (physics.MoveCharacterWater, used by physics.character): gravity is reduced by CharBuoyancy (an idle swimmer floats with the head just out), vertical speed is damped, the walking intent is scaled to SwimFactor and pushed by the current. From 30% immersion the character swims: Jump or Move.Y > 0 swims up, Move.Y < 0 dives; jumping from a pool floor is a swim up. With the head out of the water, pushing against a ledge up to one block above the surface climbs onto it.
  • State and API. CharacterController.Submerged (fraction) and HeadUnder are written each tick (hashed only when non-zero, so dry scenes keep their hashes); InWater() and HeadUnderWater() read them. app.Controller (the sandbox walker) has the same model with InWater() / HeadUnderWater() and FrameState.InWater / HeadUnder for the HUD and an underwater view.
  • Input. No new action: the default jump action swims up and the crouch / fly_down key dives (KeyDown in the sandbox).
  • Scripts. fluid_amount, fluid_pour and fluid_drain (module vox, ABI 1: additive imports) expose the same volume a pump needs: fluid.UnitsPerBlock = 64 units per full cell (FluidFull in the SDK), a source counts 64, a coarse flowing level 8, and a fine pour adds sub-cell units (rounded down to the script's unit). They return UNAVAILABLE without the automaton and INVALID outside the world or for 0 or more than 64 units. They act at once, like the physics3d calls: the returned amount is exact, a later fault of the same call does not undo the water, and fluid_amount sees the result; the water then flows by the rules above. A fine pour is entered in the Ledger (Created) and a fine drain in Displaced, so the conservation invariant still holds. A script also reads names (water, water_1..water_7) with voxel_get + block_id_by_name, a character's physics.character component with entity_get_component (submerged, head_under), and swims by setting the intent (move, jump) like walking. Machine examples (a pump and a tank): blocks.

Editor and agents#

  • The editor installs the fluid plugin by default (core.Config.NoFluids removes it). Placing water is the normal place / box tool with block water: one undo step, journaled, replayable; undo removes the source and the flow recedes when the simulation ticks.
  • Edit mode never lets water move: the simulation only ticks in Play (and editor_step, which is refused in edit mode). Stop restores the snapshot taken at Play, flowing water included, bit for bit.
  • editor_water {pos} (read only): block, level (0 none, 1..7 flowing, 8 source), height, falling, flow (unit vector) and the automaton state (idle, active_cells, pending_wakeups, dropped).
  • burrowd sessions (vox_act / vox_step) have the automaton too: set_block / fill_box with water, then step. fluid_wake is a normal command kind. vox.project runtime.plugins.fluid (default off) controls burrowplay.

For the renderer#

Read-only, pure functions of the voxels and the registry (no state):

mesh.NewLiquidTable(reg)                       -> table (rebuild after registering blocks)
mesh.LiquidHeight(t, r, p)        float32      surface height inside the cell, 0..1 (1 when water is above)
mesh.LiquidCornerHeights(t, r, p) [4]float32   top corners (-x-z, +x-z, +x+z, -x+z), smooth across cells
mesh.LiquidFlow(t, r, p)          mathx.Vec3   unit push direction (Y < 0 when falling), for ripples and foam

Block names water_1..water_7 alias the water texture and tint in assets/blocktex; Vertex.Block of a quad tells the level. The mesher itself is unchanged (a flowing cell still meshes as a full unit cube; sloping its top face is the renderer's job).

Limits#

  • No pathfinding toward holes (Minecraft spreads toward the nearest drop); no waterlogged blocks; no evaporation by weather or rain inflow (the weather module exposes RainSupply and Phase, nothing consumes them yet); lava flows only with Config.Lava (see "Flowing lava").
  • Water is a stepped automaton at 4 cells/s: no momentum, no pressure, no waves; levels are 1/8 cell.
  • A flowing cell takes the full cell for collision purposes except its surface height; characters are pushed by the average flow, not per-face.
  • Past the caps the oldest-cell guarantee is lost: shed cells (Dropped > 0) freeze until a neighbour changes or fluid_wake.
  • The rolling back of a caller-owned voxel undo recording (BeginUndo outside the engine) restores chunks without telling the automaton; engine commands, the editor and Restore are covered.
  • Compatibility. The plugin appends water_1..water_7 to the registry and adds an extra state, so a snapshot saved before it (a burrowctl state.snap, an editor Play snapshot kept on disk) no longer restores into an engine that has it; Restore says so ("a snapshot saved before an optional module such as the water flow ... cannot be restored") and the fix is to save a new one. .voxworld files and journals of the plain world still load (the registry prefix is accepted). A journal in which a character enters water replays to another hash than before this stage (it now swims); dry journals are unchanged.

Voxel size (coarse water)#

The coarse automaton works in units of 1/Scale level (fluid.Blocks.Scale = the world's Subdiv at install time): flowing water loses one unit per cell, so it spreads the same 7 m at every voxel size, and the step runs Scale passes (one cell of front each) per Interval, so the front keeps its metres per second. Each level 1..7 (the visual height, Block.Liquid) has Scale phase blocks: water_L is the full phase, water_L_p (p = 1..Scale-1) the thinner ones, so the blocks of a finer world are the 28/56 flowing blocks of Subdiv 4/8. Scale 1 registers and behaves exactly as before (a world that never leaves one metre voxels never registers a phase block). The fine water has its own section in water-fine.md.

The scale follows the voxel size at runtime. Install registers the classic water, water_1..7; the phase blocks (water_L_p, p = 1..7, for every level, one canonical order) are appended after every other block when the world needs a finer scale: by the plugin's late setup stage (a world that starts at 0.25 m), by sim.Engine.ChangeSubdiv through Engine.OnSubdiv (a scene switch: game.SceneSwitch.Apply runs it, so the growth is journaled with the command and a replay reproduces it) and, for a bare Install, at the first tick. Ids are append-only and never renumbered; the phases of a finer scale stay registered after switching back and read as their full level (water_L). The registry gets the phases of every scale at once, so it does not depend on the history (an editor that switched 1 m -> 0.25 m, a game that started at 0.25 m and a replay list the same blocks, which a .voxworld palette needs). State.rescale also repairs a snapshot restore or a rolled-back command at the next tick and fluid command. The registry is locked (sim.Engine.RegistryLock, the mesh workers' lock) while it grows on a live app.

Flowing lava (Config.Lava)#

Opt-in: fluid.Config{Lava: true} registers lava (source) and lava_1..lava_3 right after the water blocks (an existing non-solid lava block of Fluid kind lava is reused). With it off nothing is registered and every hash is unchanged; the Config hash gains the lava fields only when on. Not available with Fine (Install refuses it); the lava blocks are not Liquid blocks (no buoyancy, no flow height in meshes, which see a plain non-solid block). Emission and heat are set on the blocks (15 for the source). There is no block damage hook in the engine, so lava does not hurt by itself; scripts read the block name.

  • Speed. Lava rules run every LavaInterval ticks (default 3*Interval = 15: a third of the speed of water). A multiple of Interval. Unstable lava cells wait in the work list between lava steps.
  • Reach. A source spreads 3 cells (lava_3, lava_2, lava_1), falling lava is lava_3 and spreads again where it lands. No infinite sources. Unfed flowing lava drains to air. Fragile blocks are replaced. The reach is in voxels (not scaled by Subdiv).
  • Water onto lava. Source -> Config.CoolSource; flowing -> Config.CoolFlow; not a registered solid block: lava blocks the water.
  • Lava onto water (source or flowing, also falling onto it): the water cell becomes Config.CoolWater; not registered: the water stays and the lava does not enter. The defaults are obsidian, cobblestone and stone (blockset.DefaultLavaCooling); the names are hashed with the Config.
  • Integers only; two runs, replays and snapshots give the same StateHash (lava_test.go).

Anti-aliasing and upscaling (vendored shader sources, MIT)#

The Vulkan renderer's anti-aliasing and upscaling stages (docs/anti-aliasing.md) vendor the reference shader sources below. All three are MIT licensed; the licence texts ship next to the sources and the copyright notices stay in every file.

componentwhereversionlicence
SMAA (Subpixel Morphological Anti-Aliasing) reference implementation SMAA.hlsl and the lookup textures AreaTex.h / SearchTex.h (converted to the binary files render/vulkan/aadata/areatex.bin, searchtex.bin), by Jorge Jimenez, Jose I. Echevarria, Belen Masia, Fernando Navarro, Diego Gutierrez, (c) 2013shaders/third_party/smaa/ (licence: LICENSE.txt there and render/vulkan/aadata/SMAA_LICENSE.txt), https://github.com/iryoku/smaamaster (SMAA 2.8)MIT with the clarification that the copyright notice need not be reproduced in binary distributions
AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution 1 ffx_a.h, ffx_fsr1.h (EASU edge-adaptive spatial upsampling, RCAS contrast-adaptive sharpening), (c) 2021 Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.shaders/third_party/fsr/ (licence: LICENSE.txt there), https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSRv1.20210629MIT
AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution 2 (FSR 2.2) compute passes (luminance pyramid / SPD, reconstruct and dilate previous depth, depth clip, lock, accumulate, and the shared headers), (c) 2022-2023 Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. The host side (resources, constants, dispatch order, jitter and bias tables) is a Go port of ffx_fsr2.cpp in render/vulkan/fsr2.goshaders/third_party/fsr2/ (licence: LICENSE.txt there), https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR22.2.1MIT
  • Modifications of the vendored sources. SMAA.hlsl: line endings only (CRLF to LF). FSR 1: none. FSR 2: two format qualifiers in ffx_fsr2_callbacks_glsl.h (rw_dilatedDepth r16f to r32f and rw_prepared_input_color rgba16 to rgba16f, marked "Burrow:" in the file), so that the storage image formats match the images the SDK itself allocates (R32_FLOAT and R16G16B16A16_FLOAT; the unmodified qualifiers make the validation layer report a format mismatch). The passes are compiled by make shaders-fsr2 with the options of the SDK's Vulkan backend (HDR colour input, low resolution motion vectors, standard depth, no integrated sharpening). The SDK's RCAS and auto-reactive passes are not used.
  • FXAA (shaders/post_fxaa.frag) is an original implementation written for Burrow from the published description of the technique (luma contrast gate, edge orientation, a walk along the edge, a shift of the lookup across it); an earlier version followed NVIDIA's Fxaa3_11.h too closely and was replaced by a rewrite with its own structure, identifiers and constants. No NVIDIA source is included.
  • Not bundled: NVIDIA DLSS and Intel XeSS. -upscale dlss and -upscale xess select stubs (vulkan.Upscaler, unavailableUpscaler) that fail with a clear message and fall back to the built-in TAA. DLSS needs NVIDIA's NGX / Streamline SDK under NVIDIA's own licence, which is not redistributable here and was not downloaded; XeSS needs Intel's SDK. A backend wraps the vendor SDK behind the Upscaler interface (see docs/anti-aliasing.md) after the SDK is obtained from the vendor under its licence.

Audio assets#

The engine itself ships no sound in its mixer. The game binaries (burrowplay, Burrow.exe, voxel_sandbox) embed a default sound pack (assets/sounds/default/, removable with -tags nosounds) made of CC0 files, which may be redistributed and need no attribution; they are credited here anyway:

  • Kenney Impact Sounds, Kenney RPG Audio and Kenney Interface Sounds by Kenney, https://kenney.nl, CC0 1.0 (licence texts in assets/sounds/default/licences/): footsteps, block impacts, cloth, creaks and UI clicks (sfx/).
  • "JRPG Music Pack #4 [Calm]" by Juhani Junkala, https://opengameart.org/content/jrpg-pack-4-calm, CC0 (re-encoded to Ogg Vorbis q3): the six music tracks (music/).

Files of a user sound pack (-sounds, ~/.burrow/sounds) are never bundled and remain under their owner's rights. audio/testdata/tiny.ogg is a 0.5 s sine test tone encoded with ffmpeg's native Vorbis encoder, and audio/testdata/golden_scene.wav is the engine's own offline render (both test fixtures, MIT).
